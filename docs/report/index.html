Marking, grading and bridging 40 classical Tamil books

This report describes how the project turned the public-domain Tamil text of 40 books into a graded knowledge graph. AI agents mark each chapter against a written brief. Other agents grade every proposed link blind. The lessons from each grading round go back into the briefs. Every number below comes from a file in the repository, named under each figure.

All marking and grading was done by Claude models (Opus and Sonnet). No Tamil scholar has reviewed the results yet.

1Executive summary

The project built a knowledge graph over 40 classical Tamil books in nine groups. Every verse carries the Tamil original and an agent's own reading in English. Links between verses are proposed during marking, graded blind with hidden calibration links, and kept with their grade. An explorer draws the graph group by group and shows the bridges between groups.

40books in 9 groups
20,606verses or reading units marked
6,916marking-pass links graded blind (of 6,936)
33.8%graded sound (strictest grade kept)
0.59inter-grader kappa, sound vs not, on 2,976 grade pairs
828hidden calibration links; graders exact 65.6%

Three findings stand out.

  1. Lessons fed back into the briefs raise quality, and the effect is measurable. Akam-to-Kural links rose from 25.0% to 59.7% sound once a rule required the two verses to share the speaker's situation. Agreement on the epics' stance field rose from kappa 0.34–0.39 in the pilot to 0.76 in wave 5 after the rule for refuted was tightened.
  2. Bridges between groups are few, and most of the good ones run through the ethics works. All bridges together are 37.6% sound against 58.1% within groups. Akam to ethics and puram to ethics both reach 46%, level with their own baselines.
  3. The model matters as much as the method. Under the same Sonnet grader, Opus-marked Civaka Cintamani chapters were 18.9% sound and Sonnet-marked chapters 10.4%. The Sonnet grader was also stricter and less calibrated. The Jain epics finished at 13.5% sound and remain the weakest part of the graph.

Every text comes from Project Madurai e-texts, identified by their pmuni file numbers in docs/ingest/*.md. For the Ettuthogai, each line was aligned with the sangamtamil.com text and every difference was classed as spelling, reading, line break or eye-skip. Copyright rules

  • Only the Tamil original is stored, and it is public domain. Readings and notes are the agents' own words.
  • No modern translation or commentary is stored or quoted. The marking check fails a reading that repeats a run of six words from a modern commentary held in the core file.
  • sangamtamil.com's English translations are a translator's modern work. Only the site's Tamil lines and thinai labels were used.
  • Texts still in copyright stay on the owner's machine in data/private/, which git ignores.

3Pipeline

Each book passes through the same stages. Marking and grading are done by separate agents that see different material. The loop from scoring back to the briefs is the part of the method that changes results.

  1. Ingest. Parse each e-text into records with stable ids such as purananuru:192 or pattupattu:1:1-24.
  2. Repair. Diff against sangamtamil.com, arbitrate with tamilvu, log every change.
  3. Mark. An agent marks one chapter at a time by the brief for its field set, and proposes links with a type, a note and a teaches sentence.
  4. Check. scripts/book_marks.py check validates the schema, concept ids, verse ids and copyright rules.
  5. Packet. scripts/make_sangam_packets.py shuffles links into blind packets and hides calibration links of known grade among them.
  6. Grade and score. One grader takes every packet, a second takes a sample. scripts/score_sangam_grades.py computes agreement and calibration. Where graders differ, the strictest grade is kept.
  7. Feed back. Weak link kinds are named and a rule is written into the brief.
  8. Build. scripts/build_group_graphs.py writes the nine group graphs and the bridge matrix. scripts/export_viz.py feeds the explorer.

4Annotation schema

Every verse gets the house fields: a one-sentence reading, one to four concepts, an optional nuance and an uncertain flag. Each group then adds the fields of its field set. A field is kept only if a second blind reader can reproduce it.

The Tholkappiyam pilot: which fields are reliable

Before the Sangam books were marked, two agents tagged the same 100 verses (20 each from five books) by the Tholkappiyam brief, without seeing each other's tags. Fields with kappa of 0.8 or more were adopted. Fields near 0.5 or below were dropped or recast.

5Evaluation design

Kappa in one sentence: kappa measures how often two readers agree beyond what they would agree by chance, where 0 is chance and 1 is perfect agreement.

  • Blind packets. A grader sees the Tamil of both verses, the marked readings, the link type, its note and its teaches sentence. The grader does not see who proposed the link and is told that fluent notes are not evidence.
  • Four grades. Sound: the connection is real and teaches names something neither verse shows alone. Weak: real but thin. Forced: depends on stretching a word or reading. Wrong: misreads a verse.
  • Hidden calibration. Links whose grade is already settled are shuffled into each packet. Across all runs, 828 calibration links were graded: exact 65.6%, stricter 181 times, more lenient 104 times.
  • Second graders. 1,138 links were graded more than once, giving 2,976 grade pairs. Exact agreement was 72.5% and kappa on sound against not sound was 0.59.
  • Strictest grade wins. Where graders differ, the lower grade is kept, and every grade is recorded with its run, grader and model.
  • Field re-tags. Part B of a packet asks the grader to re-tag a field (ullurai, stance, domain) without seeing the marker's tag. That is how field reliability is measured.
  • Controls in the science comparison. 100 random verse-finding pairs go through the same packets unlabelled, and the threshold for a strong link is set from how they score (section 7).

Source: docs/sangam-grader-brief.md, kg/groups/all_link_audit.json (summary)

6.4 Stance agreement on the epics

In the pilot the marker called 47 of 62 claims refuted, and the graders called most of them reported. The rule was changed: refuted only when the work answers the claim. Stance kappa between marker and grader moved from 0.34–0.39 in the pilot to 0.76 in wave 5.

7The science comparison

This track pairs claims in the Siddhar songs with current scientific findings and keeps a pair only if it beats random pairings.

  1. Findings. 60 one-sentence paraphrases of peer-reviewed reviews and trials, each with a strength label (established, replicated, contested, speculative). Findings that contradict Siddhar claims, such as mercury toxicity and limits on lifespan, are included on purpose.
  2. Scoring. All 162,060 verse-finding pairs (2,701 × 60) are scored by keyword and concept overlap. The top 300 are selected with caps per finding and per verse.
  3. Controls. 100 negative controls (seed 20261125): a candidate finding with a random verse, or a candidate verse with a random finding.
  4. Blind grading. Two graders score all 400 items 0–3 in shuffled packets, with a direction (supports, contradicts, neither).
  5. Threshold from the controls. A pair is strong only if its mean grade is at least 2, above the 95th percentile of control grades, and its overlap is above the 95th percentile of control overlaps.

The 17 strong pairs: 8 support, 5 contradict and 4 are neither. Two depend on a cipher reading the Tamil does not fix. Six are about one finding: that mercury becomes gold only by nuclear reaction. Several of those verses themselves mock outward gold-making.

Correspondence is not confirmation. A strong pair means a blind grader judged that the verse makes a specific claim a finding bears on, more clearly than random pairings. It does not mean the Siddhars anticipated the science, that a finding confirms the verse's metaphysics, or that any practice is safe. Medical and alchemical verses are recorded as historical claims, never remedies.

8Siddhar reading layers and the cipher lexicon

The Siddhar songs often carry two senses: a plain one and a coded one about breath, alchemy or consciousness. A pilot on 6 chapters (99 verses, 6 poets) read each verse in five layers (literal, breath, alchemy, consciousness, social), proposed 41 links, and drafted a lexicon of 39 double-reading words such as வாசி, மூலநாடி and வெளி.

A negative result. The graders rated 46% and 37% of the new links sound, no better than the flat marking's 53%. Both graders said 36 of the 41 links hold without their layer. The layer describes a link more often than it carries it. Consciousness links (17%) and shared-key links (25%) were the least reliable.

What was kept.

  • The cipher lexicon v0.2: 26 entries accepted by both graders, 10 rejected, 3 disputed. Graders agreed on 92% of entries. The acceptance rate is 67%, against ThinaiKG's 83% from a single reviewer.
  • The stance field. Old links between verses of the same stance were 64% sound, against 48% across stances, and the sound "tension" links were the stance differences.
  • The body-place field, and a rule to hold consciousness and shared-key links to a stricter bar.

The 2,701 Siddhar verses were not re-marked in layers.

Source: kg/themes/siddhar_layers/findings.md

9Exploratory census of the whole Tamil corpus

A separate track asks how the texts describe knowing and measuring. It is exploratory and not part of the graded graph.

  • Census. 41,583 Tamil units in 45 corpus groups were tested against 84 registered comparisons: 3,492,972 pairs. 2,218 pairs in 1,742 units met both vocabulary conditions within three lines. The rule recovered only 1 of the 17 strong science pairs and matched 0 of 100 controls. Vocabulary alone is a poor proxy for a reading.
  • Theme combinations. Six sets of Tamil search terms were crossed over all 63 subsets. Only 289 units matched two or more. One enrichment (development with ecology, 39 units against 20.43 expected, Holm-adjusted p = 0.0285) vanished when three short ambiguous strings were removed. For example, கரு also matches the start of the word for sugarcane.

No combination had corrected evidence in both vocabulary runs. The 289 units are a reading queue, not a result.

Source: kg/science/consciousness/census.md, kg/science/consciousness/combinations.md

10Related work

ThinaiKG (M.Tech dissertation, 2025–26)

ThinaiKG classified 1,891 akam verses into the five thinai using only karu words as features. A lexicon grew from 176 to 417 terms with a human accepting each proposed term (83% accepted). A linear SVM reached macro F1 0.82; IndicBERT peaked at 58.6%. The graph is published at sangamtamil.com.

What we adopted: domain theory as features, a versioned lexicon with an acceptance loop, and double confirmation before an edge enters the graph. What we treated with caution: the 500 gold labels may be colophon labels, no held-out split is described, and one reviewer accepted the terms. Tholkappiyam lets karu cross landscapes and makes uri decide the thinai, so an entity-only model reads the weakest signal. Our marking tags uri directly.

sangamtamil.com

A static reading edition of 47 texts. We crawled it at one request per second (453 requests) and used only its Tamil lines and thinai labels: as a line-by-line cross-check of our Ettuthogai text and as a source of native-thing mentions for validation. Its English translations are modern work and were not used.

12Limitations

  • All graders are AI models. Markers and graders are Claude agents sharing training. No grader had a Tamil lexicon. Agreement between them is not the same as being right.
  • No Tamil scholar has reviewed anything yet. The readings, grades, field tags and lexicon are unreviewed.
  • Two grading regimes in one matrix. The five kept graphs (Kural, Naladiyar, Tirumanthiram, Thiruppugazh, Siddhars) were graded under older link-pass and per-book briefs. The Tirumanthiram's graders ran lenient against calibration. Their cells are not comparable with the marking pass's.
  • Weak Jain epic links. 303 of 2,242 wave-6 links are sound (13.5%). Soolamani 9%, Udayana Kumara 7%, Naga Kumara 7%. Most of this is the Sonnet marking and grading.
  • 20 links never graded: 8 in Paripadal and 12 in Pattupattu were in no packet.
  • Low agreement on some books: Valayapathi kappa 0.04, Soolamani 0.15. Their sound% is unsettled.
  • Concept merges are judgment calls. Four of the twelve aliases join different Tamil words and could be split again.
  • Modern commentary is kept local only. Copyrighted translations and commentary sit in the git-ignored data/private/ and were not used for readings, so the graph lacks the commentary tradition except for the Kural and Naladiyar.
  • Token cost was not recorded per wave. Only the early Kural link pass has a figure (about 1.2 million tokens per 443 links).

13Reproduction

All scripts are standard-library Python, run from the repository root with python3 -I.

# Text: parse and repair

python3 -I scripts/repair_ettuthogai.py      # tamilvu arbitration for three anthologies

# Marking: show and validate a book's chapters
python3 -I scripts/book_marks.py purananuru show 3
python3 -I scripts/book_marks.py purananuru check 3

# Grading: packets, then scores
python3 -I scripts/make_sangam_packets.py
python3 -I scripts/score_sangam_grades.py

# Merge grades and build the graphs
python3 -I scripts/merge_sangam_grades.py    # kg/groups/*/link_audit.json, all_link_audit.json
python3 -I scripts/merge_concepts.py         # kg/themes/concepts.json
python3 -I scripts/build_group_graphs.py     # nodes, edges, bridges.jsonl, bridges_summary.json

# Side tracks

python3 -I scripts/science_pairs.py

14Glossary

akam / puram அகம் / புறம்
The inner life of love and the outer life of kings, war, gifts and death: the two halves of Sangam poetry.
thinai திணை
A poetic landscape and the mode of love or conduct it stands for. Akam has five (kurinji, mullai, marutham, neytal, palai).
mutal முதல்
The first things of a poem: its land, season and hour.
karu கரு
The native things of a landscape: its plants, animals, people, gods and tools.
uri உரி
The human situation proper to a thinai, such as union, separation or waiting. In the Tholkappiyam it settles the thinai.
thurai துறை
The speech situation of a poem: who speaks, to whom, on what occasion. Usually given in the colophon.
meyppadu மெய்ப்பாடு
The feeling a verse displays, one of eight, with its cause.
ullurai உள்ளுறை
A hidden comparison: a scene from the landscape that stands for the human situation without saying so. Experimental field.
iraicchi இறைச்சி
A sense that rises from a native thing, beyond its literal role. Tagged only when removing the thing would lose that sense. Experimental field.
kappa
Agreement between two readers beyond chance: 0 is chance, 1 is perfect.
sound
A real connection with the right type, an accurate note, and a teaches sentence that says something neither verse says alone.
weak
A real connection that is thin, generic or overstated.
forced
A connection that depends on stretching a word, a reading or a colophon.
wrong
A link that misreads a verse or claims something the verses contradict.
calibration link
A link of settled grade hidden in a packet to check the grader.
