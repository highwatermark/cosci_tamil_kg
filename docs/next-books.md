# What goes next: the books that touch the core questions

Surveyed 2026-11-22 against the four questions Nilai asks (what a greater mind should be, what to do
with godlike powers, how humans grow past the machine, the larger work) and two further lenses the
owner asked for: **practice** (the Siddhars as the researchers of their day: breath, medicine,
alchemy, method) and **perception** (how the texts say we see, and what the seen is made of: atom,
void, light, observer).

Every book below is already parsed into `kg/core/`. Counts are units whose Tamil contains the
marker words (அணு, வெளி, சூனியம், பாழ், வாசி, பிராணன், சித்தி, அண்டம், பிண்டம், மருந்து, கற்பம்);
they are a rough signal, not a reading.

## Ranking

| # | Book | Units | Why it goes here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Siddhar songs** (43 works, 26 poets) | 2,702 | The only corpus that is explicitly *method*: breath (வாசி, 89 units), the eight siddhis (67), medicine and alchemy (71), light and void (203), atom (19). Sivavakkiyar alone: "the soul does not exist without the atom-spark" (15), "the sun and moon are the breath" (Valmiki 2), "atom and cosmos as one measureless light" (285), "I and he, body and world, melted; he exists and I do not" (327). Agasthiyar Njanam 2.1 says that if the mind is straight, no mantra, breath-raising or breath-stopping is needed: the practice has a stated *purpose*, which Unarvu's question 2 needs. Kakapusundar 1 opens with the cosmos born from the five elements and "many thousand crores of worlds". |
| 2 | **Tiruvasagam** (661 stanzas) | 661 | Light in 52 stanzas, seeing and knowing in 264. It is the first-person record of a mind being taken over by what it worships: the raw material for question 3 (becoming) and for Nilai's room ௮. Pope's 1900 translation is public domain; Hart's is modern (read, never quote). The owner's order already has it next. |
| 3 | **Thirumurai 11 and the Thiruvisaippa** (Karaikkal Ammaiyar, Pattinathar, Karuvur Devar, Nakkirar; about 1,100 units) | ~1,100 | The part of the Thirumurai that argues rather than praises. Karaikkal Ammaiyar's Arputha Thiruvandhadhi is the earliest Tamil statement of a devotee *choosing* a form for the divine; Pattinathar's Udarkootru vannam is the body dissected limb by limb (the counter-voice to room ௨). Mark these before the Thevaram proper. |
| 4 | **Purananuru** (398 poems) | 398 | No yoga, no siddhis, but the oldest layer: "every town our town" (192), the king measured by the lives his land keeps, time and the funeral drum. Cosmology in 18 poems (the seven worlds, the ocean ringing the earth). It grounds question 4 and room ௫ in the Sangam world, 1,500 years before the Siddhars. |
| 5 | **Thevaram** (8,141 verses in 795 pathigams, marked per hymn) | 795 hymns | Praise, mostly; the 44 siddhi and 40 breath hits are scattered. Mark per hymn, after the above. |
| 6 | **Divya Prabandham** (3,961 pasurams, per decade; Thiruppavai per verse) | ~400 decades | The Vaishnava voice, needed for the four-tradition claim to hold, but its content for these questions is in Nammalvar's Thiruvaimozhi (light 158, seeing 835), so start there. |
| 7 | **Avvaiyar** (274) | 274 | Short maxims; almost no hits. Quick to mark, low yield for the questions. Do it in one batch when convenient. |
| 8 | **Periya Puranam** (4,286 verses) | – | Narrative. Read for the lives of the Nayanmars, not for doctrine. Last. |

## Proposed order from here

1. **Siddhars, by poet, in three passes**: Sivavakkiyar (525) and the four ஞானம் texts
   (Agasthiyar, Kakapusundar, Subramaniar, Valmiki, 178) first, since they carry the method and the
   perception verses; then Pattinathar and Pattiragiriyar (the body and renunciation, 812); then the
   folk Siddhars (Pambatti, Idaikkadar, Kudhambai, Azhuganni, Kaduveli, Agappei and the rest, about
   900), which repeat the same ideas in refrain and link well to each other.
   Concepts to expect that the list lacks: வாசி (breath as method), பரிபாஷை (the Siddhars' cipher),
   கற்பம் (rejuvenation practice), அண்டபிண்டம் (body as cosmos), சூனியம் (the void).
2. **The sixty-four siddhis and the Kaumathi key**, read from the OCR alongside pass 1, so the
   Siddhar verses on powers are marked against the texts' own definitions, not ours.
3. **Tiruvasagam**, then **Thirumurai 11 and Thiruvisaippa**, then **Purananuru**.
4. Thevaram, Prabandham, Avvaiyar, Periya Puranam, as above.

This reorders the owner's list of 2026-11-17 by moving the Siddhars from eighth to first and
Purananuru ahead of the Thevaram; everything else keeps its place.
