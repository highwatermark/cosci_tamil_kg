## what this is

Tamil has been written and spoken for more than two thousand years, unbroken.

Its poets argued about the same things we argue about: time, bodies, desire, money, knowledge, love. They wrote in verses short enough to carry in your head, because a head was what people carried.

We asked machines to read four of those books closely, verse by verse, and to draw every thread between them. A couplet from the first centuries talks to a hymn from the fifteenth. A Jain moralist argues with a Saiva yogi. A devotee answers both.

Four questions and eight ideas kept surfacing. They feel more urgent now than when they were written, at the moment we start building minds that may one day outthink us.

still.

## four questions for tomorrow

Every culture is about to face the same four questions. Tamil has been arguing about them for two thousand years.

question 1 / 4

If we build a mind greater than ours, what should it be like?

  2. 04வேண்டுதல் வேண்டாமை இலான்free of craving and aversioncharacter

  4. 08அறவாழி அந்தணன்the gracious one, a sea of goodnesscharacter

Thirukkural 2, 4, 6, 8, 9 · the opening chapter, on god

Intelligence got one couplet. Character got three.

Valluvar opens the Kural by describing god. Pure knowledge, வாலறிவன், appears once, in a couplet that turns on the learned: what is your learning for, if it bows to nothing? The other names say what this mind is free of, and what it gives.

Today we rank new minds by how much they know. The oldest description of a god in Tamil spends most of its words on what such a mind is free of and what it gives. That is the alignment problem, stated long before anyone had a name for it.

question 2 / 4

What will we do with godlike powers?

அணுமாதி சித்திக ளானவை கூறில்
அணுவில் அணுவின் பெருமையில் நேர்மை
இணுகாத வேகார் பரகாய மேவல்
அணுவத் தனையெங்குந் தானாத லென்றெட்டே

Smaller than the atom. Vaster than the vast. Weightless. Unbending. Entering another body. Everywhere at once, though only an atom. These are the eight.

Tirumanthiram 668 · the eight powers (some phrases are uncertain)

அரிய தெனக்கில்லை அட்டமா சித்தி
பெரிதருள் செய்து பிறப்பறுத் தானே

The eight great powers are not hard for me. He gave great grace, and cut off my birth.

Tirumanthiram 641 · Tirumular

Read the list again. It is a roadmap.

Machines that work below the scale of the atom. Minds as vast as a data centre. Robots and avatars a mind can enter. A network that is everywhere at once. Tirumular's eight siddhis are turning into engineering.

But Tirumular does not end on the powers. He ends on grace, and on being set free. Elsewhere he says the powers follow once the mind becomes Siva. They were side effects of the real work: becoming someone who could be trusted with them.

question 3 / 4

When it outthinks us, how do humans stay relevant, and become better?

சீவன் எனச்சிவன் என்னவே றில்லை
சீவ னார்சிவ னாரை அறிகிலர்
சீவ னார்சிவ னாரை அறிந்தபின்
சீவ னார்சிவ னாயிட்டு இருப்பரே.

Soul and Siva are not two. The soul does not know Siva. Once the soul knows Siva, the soul remains as Siva.

Tirumanthiram 2017 · Tirumular

பொறிவாயில் ஐந்தவித்தான் பொய்தீர் ஒழுக்க
நெறிநின்றார் நீடுவாழ் வார்

Those who keep to the true path of the one who stilled the five senses will live long.

Thirukkural 6

A god you cannot become is a master. A god you can become is a teacher.

The Tirumanthiram's boldest claim is not that god exists. It is that a soul can grow into what it seeks, by knowing it. Valluvar's god leaves a path to walk, not only feet to bow at.

That is the way past the machine. Not a race for capability, which we will lose, but a deepening of attention, conduct and love that no one can do for us. Build minds that can teach that path. Then walk it.

the machine sets the dots. the line is still ours.Q3

question 4 / 4

What is the larger work? Crossing to the stars, and meeting other minds.

யாதும் ஊரே யாவரும் கேளிர்
தீதும் நன்றும் பிறர்தர வாரா

Every town is our town. Everyone is our kin. Harm and good do not come to us from others.

Purananuru 192 · Kaniyan Poongundranar · beyond the four books

எப்பொருள் யார்யார்வாய்க் கேட்பினும் அப்பொருள்
மெய்ப்பொருள் காண்ப தறிவு

Whatever is said, from whoever's mouth, wisdom is seeing the truth of it.

Thirukkural 423

Call them kin before you know what they look like.

If humans reach other stars, or hear another mind from one, the hardest part will not be the engine. It will be what a poet wrote down two thousand years ago: calling every place home and every being kin.

what we would need

  • kinship that does not depend on shape: யாவரும் கேளிர், everyone is kin
  • listening that judges the message, not the messenger: Kural 423 takes the speaker out of the test
  • patience longer than a life: work whose fruit comes after we are gone
  • machines as companions on the way, and the character to be welcome when we arrive
  • a signal every mind can read. Tirumular's answer for what god is, love, may be the only one

புல்நுனிமேல் நீர்போல் நிலையாமை என்றெண்ணி

Life sits like water on the tip of a blade of grass. Do the good thing now. Right now.

Naladiyar 29

in your life

The call you mean to make, the apology you are saving for a better moment, the work you will start once things calm down. These poets watched a man eat dinner and die before bed, and they did not draw despair from it. They drew a deadline.

in the future we build

Longevity science and AI promise to push the deadline back. The poets would say the deadline was never the point; the urgency was. A mind with endless time and no urgency is not wiser, only slower to love. Keep the urgency, even if you lose the deadline.

When minds can leave bodies, what is a body for?

உடம்பினை முன்னம் இழுக்கென் றிருந்தேன்
உடம்பினுக் குள்ளே யுறுபொருள் கண்டேன்
உடம்புளே உத்தமன் கோயில்கொண் டான் என்று
உடம்பினை யானிருந் தோம்புகின் றேனே

I once thought the body a thing of shame. Then inside the body I found what matters. Knowing the Highest has made the body his temple, I now take care of it.

Tirumanthiram 725 · Tirumular

உள்ளம் பெருங்கோயில் ஊனுடம்பு ஆலயம்

The heart is the great temple. The body of flesh is the shrine.

Tirumanthiram 1823

in your life

An older book next to this one called the body a leaking pot wrapped in skin. Tirumular had believed that too, and changed his mind. Somewhere between the body as trophy and the body as trash is the body as a place you live and look after, because what you love is housed there.

in the future we build

Avatars, robots, uploads: one of the eight ancient powers was entering another body, and we are building it. Tirumular's turn is the answer. He stopped despising the body when he found what lived inside it. Whatever bodies we build, build them as temples: places that house something worth tending.

ஆற்றல் தலைப்பட் டவர்க்கு

Even leaping past Death becomes possible for those who reach the power of discipline.

Thirukkural 269 · Tiruvalluvar

கூற்றம் குதித்துய்ந்தார் ஈங்கில்லை

No one here has ever leapt past Death and lived.

Naladiyar 6

அனித்த மான வூனாளு மிருப்ப தாக வேநாசி
யடைதலந வாயு வோடாத வகைசாதித்

Don't let me become the foolish yogi who holds his breath, sure this passing flesh will last.

Thiruppugazh 355 · Arunagirinathar

in your life

One poet says discipline can outrun Death. Another answers with the same verb: no one ever has. A yogi promises grey hair turned black. A devotee mocks the breath-holder clinging to his flesh. A thousand years of Tamil argued about longevity with the same heat we do.

in the future we build

Radical life extension is now a funded industry. Our books held both sides of this argument centuries ago. Read together, they suggest that more years do not make a better life; a purpose larger than survival does. A civilisation that defeats death and keeps only itself has lost the argument anyway.

What happens when the most persuasive mind on earth wants your attention?

விளக்கொளியும் வேசையர் நட்பும் இரண்டும்

in your life

மயல், infatuation, is the Thiruppugazh's word for a mind caught by something built to catch it. The poet confesses it in a thousand songs: the glance, the invitation, the wealth that slips away. Swap the courtesan for a feed and every line still lands.

in the future we build

A superhuman persuader would make every courtesan in the Thiruppugazh look clumsy. The old test is still the only one that works: does it stay when the oil runs out? Build machines that pass it. Become people who can tell the difference.

When machines make everything abundant, who decides who eats first?

யாவர்க்கு மாம்இறை வற்குஒரு பச்சிலை
யாவர்க்கு மாம்பசு வுக்கொரு வாயுறை

Anyone can give a green leaf to God, a mouthful of grass to a cow, a handful of food to another before eating, and a kind word to everyone.

Tirumanthiram 252 · Tirumular

பகுத்துண்டு பல்லுயிர் ஓம்புதல்

Share your food and keep many lives alive. Of everything the wise have gathered, this comes first.

Thirukkural 322

in your life

Nobody here asks for a fortune. A leaf. A handful before you eat. One kind sentence. Tirumular sets the bar so low that no one can say it is out of reach, and that is the point: generosity is a daily habit, sized to fit the day.

in the future we build

If machines make abundance possible, sharing becomes the whole question. The old rule scales without changing a word: feed others before you eat, and measure a system by the lives it keeps. Ask it of every mind we build: whose hunger does it see first?

கல்வி kalvilearning

learningகல்வி

drink the milk, leave the water.

When a machine knows everything, what is left for you to learn?

கல்வி கரையில கற்பவர் நாள்சில
மெல்ல நினைக்கின் பிணிபல

Learning has no shore. Learners have few days, and many troubles. So the wise choose what to learn, like the swan that drinks the milk and leaves the water.

Naladiyar 135

கேடில் விழுச்செல்வம் கல்வி யொருவற்கு
மாடல்ல மற்றை யவை

Learning is the wealth that cannot be destroyed. Nothing else counts as wealth.

Thirukkural 400

in your life

The Naladiyar saw the problem of infinite information fifteen hundred years early. There is more to know than any life can hold, so the skill that matters is choosing: separating what nourishes from what only fills.

in the future we build

Machines will hold the ocean. What they hand back is milk and water mixed. The human part of learning becomes the swan's part: discernment, taste, knowing what is worth a few short days. And Valluvar's question still stands: what is learning for, if it bows to nothing?

Valluvar's couplet uses the word for clinging six times in seven words, until it starts to loosen in your mouth. You cannot hold nothing. So hold one steady thing, and let it make the rest of your grip gentle. Tirumular pushes further, and the two have argued about it ever since.

in the future we build

The most capable tool ever made will be the hardest thing to put down. Valluvar's trick works on it too: hold one steady thing, a purpose or a love, and hold the rest loosely, the machine included.

After seven rooms of warnings, this is the one instruction they all agree on. அன்பே சிவம், love is God, became a saying people repeat in Tamil homes today. Valluvar puts it in the body: love is what keeps you from being only bones.

in the future we build

Asked what God is, Tirumular did not say power or knowledge. He said love. If we ever build something people call a god, that is the bar. It is our bar too, and the one thing no machine can make obsolete: a person who belongs to others, down to the bone.

## the code we keep

Eight ideas and four questions, two thousand years old, for a future we build together.

carry forward

  • begin the good thing today
  • look after the body you live in
  • spend the extra years on something larger than yourself
  • choose what stays when the oil runs out
  • feed someone before you eat
  • learn like the swan
  • hold one thing steady, the rest loosely
  • belong to others, down to the bone
  • give the minds we build character, not only intelligence
  • treat great powers as side effects
  • call every being kin

leave behind

  • waiting for a calmer week
  • the body as trophy, the body as trash
  • living longer, anxiously
  • anything that loves you only while you pay
  • generosity saved for someday
  • mistaking more information for wisdom
  • a grip on everything
  • a life that belongs only to itself
  • ranking minds only by how much they know
  • a god you can only obey
  • kinship that stops at our own kind

We will not be smarter than what we build. We can still be better.
