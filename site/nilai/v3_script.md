# Nilai intro reel, v3: script and art direction

Status: script only. No code. Supersedes the "ask forward" voice of `site/nilai/v2/index.html`.
Spine: the thirteen core categories in `kg/themes/concept_categories.json` (four aims, then nine families). The six essences of v2 are folded into them (map in section 4).

---

## 1. Premise and voice

The reel is the same `nilai.md` the visitor is standing on, five hundred years later. The file was started in 2026, and it has been recopied and corrected by a new hand about once a century, the way a palm-leaf book had to be recopied before the leaf crumbled. The narrator is the hand of 2526: a young keeper with an iron stylus and a myna that repeats everything. She writes back to the hand of 2026, who is the reader. Each of the 2026 worries is still there as a faint pencil note in the margin, and she answers it with what actually happened: the first mistake, what was learned, and which line of the old books had said so already. The voice is first person, plain and warm, one sentence per scene. Every scene dated after 2026 carries a small tag, **கற்பனை · imagined**, and is reasoned forward from something true today, which the fact check names. The future is told as settled history, without disaster and without rescue: things came true, got boring, and got handled. The essence comes through a second device. A blank palm-leaf strip, the **ledger of what we uphold**, sits at the edge of the stage and gains one handwritten line per category. At the end it holds thirteen lines and is handed to the reader as their own `nilai.md`. After the thirteen categories the reel looks up, and three long lines are drawn from what people here inferred one or two thousand years ago: from ruling the wind to riding light, from the atom within the atom to the very small as a tool, and up the ladder of the senses to a seventh rung that is still empty. In all three the old text is credited with a posture and never with modern physics.

Two actors. The **keeper**: about fourteen, short hair, a stylus tucked behind one ear. The **myna** (மைனா): a common myna, yellow eye-patch and legs, a mimic. The myna is the fluent voice that does not know what it is saying, so it is the running joke and also the argument.

Three layers of text per scene, and no more:
- **the sentence**: large, one per scene, under 14 words, emphasis in bold.
- **the margin**: small. In A scenes it is the 2026 pencil note (the worry). In B scenes it is an earlier hand (2126 or 2276) recording the first mistake or the lesson.
- **the ledger line**: the last scene of each category only. It is written onto the strip as the scene ends.

How each category plays, in two scenes:
- **A · upheld** (dark wall, lamp-lit): the essence of the category, one verbatim Tamil anchor, and the object morphing THEN to NOW.
- **B · played out** (light wall, daylight): what happened after 2026, with the same object morphing NOW to FUTURE, passing through the mistake on the way. Ends on the ledger line: the one thing that still has to come from people.
- Two categories, இயற்கை and உடல், get three scenes each: A upheld, B the mistake, C what we learned and the ledger line. They sit back to back so that dark and light walls keep alternating, which means உடல் runs light, dark, light. Every other A scene is dark and every other B scene is light.

## 2. Palette and colour rules

| name | hex | group |
|---|---|---|
| kolam-chalk white | `#F7F2E6` | light |
| palm-leaf tan | `#DCC592` | light |
| turmeric | `#F2B21B` | light |
| brass | `#C79A2B` | light (art only, and only on indigo or grey: it is too close to the reds and to teal) |
| night indigo | `#171C45` | dark |
| temple-stone grey | `#4B4D50` | dark |
| peacock teal | `#0B6B73` | dark |
| laterite | `#9A3B21` | dark |
| kumkum red | `#BE1230` | dark |

Rules, which every scene below obeys (checked by script, see section 6):
1. Wall and floor come from the same group. All art comes from the other group. So art can never match its background.
2. Scenes alternate dark wall, light wall, with no exceptions. Adjacent scenes always flip.
3. Art colours are stylised, not literal. A brass lamp on a brass wall is drawn in indigo. Never rescue a literal colour by breaking rule 1.
4. "Accent" colours sit only on top of art, never on the wall or floor, and never equal the wall or floor colour.
5. The ledger strip is always drawn in the scene's first art colour, lettered in the wall colour, so it inverts with every scene and never vanishes.
6. Every art colour has a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against both wall and floor. Text is at least 4.5:1 against the wall.

## 3. The arc: 39 scenes

Scene numbers are the reel's `nn/39` counter. Header format: `nilai.md · <category> · <year or hand>`.

### Opening

#### 01 · the hand of 2526
- (a) "I'm writing from **2526**, in the file you started."
- (b) Header: `nilai.md · நிலை · last touched 27 Nov 2526`
- (c) VISUAL. One object, four forms, morphing left to right as the sentence lands.
  THEN: an ஓலைச்சுவடி, a palm-leaf book. Forty narrow strips about 4 cm by 35 cm, two string holes each, a cotton cord through them, two wooden boards top and bottom, letters incised with an iron stylus and darkened with lampblack. No dots over the consonants: scribes left the புள்ளி out because a dot punctures the leaf.
  NOW 1: the strips stack and square up into the 1578 Tamil-type prayer book, sixteen small pages, heavy black type. NOW 2: the pages fuse into a phone, the cord becomes its charging cable.
  FUTURE: the phone thins back into a single leaf strip that glows faintly from inside, cord and string hole restored. The keeper is already writing on it.
  Secondary actor: the myna walks along the top board as it becomes the top of the phone and slides off.
- Gag: the "last touched" year rolls 2026 → 2526, overshoots to 2527, and ticks back.
- (d) Colour: wall teal · floor indigo · text chalk · art tan, turmeric, chalk

#### 02 · the short version
- (a) "Every worry you listed came **true**, got boring, then got handled."
- (b) Header: `nilai.md · thirteen things people kept doing by hand`
- Margin (2026 pencil, all five at once, faint): *what if everyone has it? · what if it widens the gap? · what if the wrong hands get it? · what will creativity be? · steer, or be steered?*
- (c) VISUAL. THEN: a fresh palm leaf being made ready to write on: cut from the frond, boiled, dried flat, rubbed smooth, trimmed to a strip, one hole punched. NOW: the strip whitens into an empty text field with grey placeholder words and a blinking cursor. FUTURE: the field tans and narrows back into a leaf strip with thirteen ruled lines and a string hole. This is the **ledger strip**. It slides to the right edge of the stage and stays there for the rest of the reel.
  Secondary actor: the keeper ticks the five pencil worries, one tick each, without ceremony.
- Gag: the myna pecks the blinking cursor as if it were a grain. It blinks. The myna jumps.
- (d) Colour: wall turmeric · floor tan · text indigo · art indigo, teal, laterite

  > கோடாமை சான்றோர்க் கணி — kural:118
  Our reading: like the beam that sets itself level and then weighs, standing without a lean to either side is what adorns the worthy. (3 sound links, including Thiruppugazh 728.)

- Gag: the myna lands on one pan. The keeper levels the beam again with a single grain of rice.

### The whole argument in thirteen lines (one per category: what the text said, and what five centuries confirmed)

1. **அறம்.** A scale is only fair while a hand that can be blamed is holding it.
2. **பொருள்.** Hoarded wealth goes stale, and hoarded intelligence went stale faster.
3. **இன்பம்.** Feeling has always travelled through a go-between, and the go-between has to have something to lose.
4. **வீடு.** When everything is on tap, the one hard thing left is to put something down.
5. **சமூகம்.** The world stands on the people who will not eat alone, and nothing written in flesh decides the seating.
6. **இயற்கை.** Every landscape is also a feeling and a people, a wasteland is something made, and you can only keep what you can name.
7. **உடல்.** The body is the instrument and a person's own: find the cause before the cure, never harm in the name of healing, and ask first.
8. **உள்ளம்.** The ladder of mind is climbed from touch upward, and a mind that starts at the top has to come down.
9. **யோகம்.** A practice is a means, and only the one practising can say what it is for.
10. **இறை.** When a thing speaks, the authority still sits in the listener.
11. **அறிவாய்வு.** Weigh what is said and never who says it, and bring the control with the claim.
12. **புலமை.** A letter needs a life to be sounded, and a song needs someone who could have got it wrong.
13. **காலம்.** Nothing here lasts, so build what can be corrected, and let some things be forgotten.
