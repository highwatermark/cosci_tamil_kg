verse 1 of 3

சிவவாக்கியம் 494 · சிவவாக்கியர் (Sivavakkiyar)

நட்டகல்லைத் தெய்வம் என்று நாலுபுட்பம் சாத்தியேசுற்றிவந்து முணமுணேன்று சொல்லுமந்திரம் ஏதடாநட்டகல்லும் பேசுமோ நாதன்உள் இருக்கையில்!சுட்டசட்டி சட்டுவம் கறிச்சுவை அறியுமோ?

the graph’s reading

Calling a planted stone a god, laying four flowers on it, circling it and muttering, what mantra is this? Will the planted stone speak while the Lord is within? Does the fired pot or the ladle know the taste of the curry?

manimekalai:21:112-129 ↔ siddhar:1458

the link’s note

Sivavakkiyar mocks the planted stone called a god and asks whether it will speak while the Lord is within; the pillar deity grants that stone and wood do not speak but says deities the wise install in them stay and speak to men.

the graph’s reading

The deity tells her not to falter before the materialist but to affirm that evil deeds reach their doer and the dead are reborn; then it explains its own speech: wood, earth, stone and painted images do not speak, but the wise, planning the protection of a guarded city, have craftsmen shape the deities they intend in clay, stone, wood and wall at its streets, temples, old trees, ghats and halls, and those deities never leave their places and tell men of fleshly eyes what will befall.

'You have won the beloved who gladdens your heart; rise, do not stand so that your slender waist aches': the king's friend spoke this trick, and the spear-eyed one, not knowing it, took it that the god she worshipped had spoken with its own mouth, and praised it and went home.
