Virtue and conduct

the balance that levels itself

அறம் · verse 1 of 3

புறநானூறு 6 · Purananuru 6

உருவும் புகழும் ஆகி, விரிசீர்த்தெரிகோல் ஞமன்ன் போல, ஒரு திறம்பற்றல் இலியரோ! நின் திறம் சிறக்க!

lines 8–10 of 1–29

the graph’s reading

Karikizhar wishes the Pandya Mudukudumi, whose form and fame reach from beyond the Himalaya to below Kumari, from sea to sea and from the waters below to the world above, that he may lean to no side like the beam of a balance, that his umbrella bow only in circling the three-eyed god's shrine, his head only before the raised hands of the Vedic sages, his garland wilt only in the smoke of burned enemy lands and his anger yield only to his women's sulking faces, and that he abide on earth like the cool moon and the burning sun.

Then the court that speaks right, which puts away fear, grief and desire, and keeps from both hatred and delight, being straight like the beam of a balance; the kaviti officers with sandal on their chests, who like the great ones who move in the sky examine good and evil and restrain them, keep love and right, shun blame and are full of fame; and the merchants who never stray from right, in many-roomed houses like a cluster of hills where kites perch and fly, rich with goods and food, selling things of hill, land and water and gems, pearls and gold from excellent countries.

பொருள் · verse 1 of 3

புறநானூறு 35 · Purananuru 35

கண்பொர விளங்கும்நின் விண்பொரு வியன்குடைவெயில்மறைக் கொண்டன்றோ? அன்றே; வருந்தியகுடிமறைப் பதுவே; கூர்வேல் வளவ!

lines 19–21 of 1–34

the graph’s reading

The poet tells Killivalavan that among the three Tamil kings kingship is his and the Kaveri-fed land is his, then counsels him: in the land of the just sceptre where the humble can reach the king when they seek justice, rain comes when it is sought; his tall umbrella is not for shade but to shelter suffering subjects; his victories are the fruit of the plough's furrow; the world blames kings when rains fail or harvests drop, so he should ignore the talk of malcontents, bear the burden of those who rear the plough-oxen and protect his people, and even the unsubmitting will bow at his feet.

the graph’s reading

The friend tells the hero, addressed as a king whose rightness keeps the rains coming as a mother watches her infant: you hold your parasol as the shade of virtue; did she fall outside its shade, she whose brow is pale with longing? your sceptre proclaims truth; did she fall outside its working, she who is weary of living from love's sickness? your drum proclaims protection; is she beyond it, whose bamboo-smooth shoulders are losing their beauty? Like an eye that sees far things but not its own scar, has it kept you from seeing that leaving my friend was cruel?
