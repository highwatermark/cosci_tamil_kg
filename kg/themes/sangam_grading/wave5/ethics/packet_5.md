## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:373 -> kural:1009 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 1009 says others take wealth amassed without love or virtue; Pazhamozhi 373 names the taker, the seizing king, and sets the small gift to the destitute against the hoard.
Teaches: The Kural leaves the taker as 'others'; Pazhamozhi makes him the king who drives the hoarder out, and pairs the lost hoard with rain in the wilderness, so the choice is between feeding royal power and feeding the destitute.
List adds: it sets two ends of wealth side by side, the hoard going to the king and a single gift going to the starving, which the couplet's one fate does not contrast.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:378 -> kural:228 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 228 asks whether the hard-eyed who hoard and lose do not know the joy of giving; Pazhamozhi 378 asks whether those who hide wealth fearing it will dwindle have not seen the well refill.
Teaches: Both ask the hoarder 'do they not know', but the Kural points to the joy he misses and Pazhamozhi to the fact he misjudges, that drawing renews the source, so the hoarder is wrong about both pleasure and arithmetic.
List adds: its well proverb supplies the hoarder's own reason, fear of dwindling, and answers it with a fact of nature the couplet does not use.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:86 -> kural:579 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 579 ranks highest the disposition to show kindly regard and bear with those whose way is to hurt; Pazhamozhi 86 sets that regard at one moment, when an open and dangerous enemy is in distress.
Teaches: The pairing fixes when the Kural's demand is hardest: not under the enemy's attack but at his fall, when sparing him is a choice and he may still bite.
List adds: the snake proverb names the enemy as still dangerous and the assembly of the worthy as the place he is spared, a setting and a risk the couplet leaves out.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:4 -> kural:397 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 397 says every land and town is the learned man's own; Pazhamozhi 4 says the same and closes with the proverb that he needs no food for the road.
Teaches: The Kural states belonging and asks why anyone stays unlearned; Pazhamozhi turns belonging into maintenance, the learned eat wherever they go.
List adds: the proverb carries the couplet's claim of belonging into the traveller's practical case, provision for the journey.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:219 -> kural:1002 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 1002 says the delusion of clinging without giving brings a மாணாப் பிறப்பு; Pazhamozhi 219 says those who give nothing for the coming birth hoard by attachment and die.
Teaches: The Kural names the bad birth as the fruit of hoarding; Pazhamozhi makes giving the provision for that birth, so not giving is a failure to pack for a journey.
List adds: it frames giving as something owed to the birth that follows (படரும் பிறப்பு) and names the attachment (பற்று) that holds the wealth, and its proverb sets outer guarding against inner loss.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:40 -> naladiyar:189 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Naladiyar 189 says the pure in heart stay unshaken by cruel slander like a flame in a lamp; Pazhamozhi 40 says undeserved reproach is a disease that, untreated by excellence of character, destroys a man.
Teaches: The Naladiyar makes the pure immune to slander; Pazhamozhi denies immunity and asks for treatment, so worth must act against blame rather than merely survive it.
List adds: its medical frame of disease, medicine and wasting gives reproach a course and a cure, where the quatrain gives only the steady flame.
