## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:131 -> kural:808 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 808 praises those whose intimacy is strong enough not to listen to reports of a friend's lapse; Pazhamozhi 131 says one does not burn against a friend one has made even for faults seen with one's own eyes.
Teaches: The Kural guards the friend against hearsay; Pazhamozhi goes further, to faults one has witnessed, and grounds the restraint in having made the friend oneself, as one does not watch one's own calf die.
List adds: its proverb of the dying calf extends the couplet's case from reported faults to seen ones and gives the reason, that the friend is one's own.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:323 -> kural:428 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 428 calls not fearing what should be feared folly and fearing it the work of the knowing; Pazhamozhi 323 denies that the fool's daring, resting on ignorance of dangers, is daring, and grants that knowing brings great fear.
Teaches: The couplet makes fear a mark of wisdom in general; Pazhamozhi carries it to the soldier and draws the hard consequence, that the brave man is one who knows the danger and so feels the fear, making courage a matter of holding against it rather than lacking it.
List adds: it moves the claim into the soldier's domain and turns it against a kind of courage, ruling out the fearless fool as brave, which the couplet does not say.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:200 -> naladiyar:280 type=TENSION
Note: Naladiyar 280 counts guarding wealth among its bitter sufferings (காத்தலும் ஆங்கே கடுந்துன்பம்); Pazhamozhi 200 says the wealth one guards guards its owner in return, like the thicket and the tiger. The tension holds on the plain reading of both: the Jain quatrain treats guarding as a cost, the proverb as a mutual defence.
Teaches: The two books judge the same act of keeping wealth oppositely: for the Naladiyar the guard is one more place of suffering, for Pazhamozhi it is what makes wealth a shelter, so the dispute is whether wealth is a burden on its owner or a defence of him.
List adds: its proverb makes guarding reciprocal, which turns the quatrain's one-way burden into an exchange of protection.

Marked reading: The one who ruled the world, thinking the gift easy for him, poured the gift-water lavishly without knowing the measure of the one who had come to him, and lost it all, for those who begin a thing with a flaw in it there is no affliction that they have not brought on themselves.
Marker's nuance: The heavy lengthenings (ஆஅம், மேஎம், தோஒம், தாஅம்) give the verse its sound. The ruler pours water (நீர் பெய்து) as a giver seals a gift; his fault is not giving but giving without weighing the recipient, so the verse fits its heading as trust given without examination.
Marker's fields: domain: king (a ruler giving a gift)

### kural:510: Kural 510
Tamil: தேரான் தெளிவும் தெளிந்தான்கண் ஐயுறவும் / தீரா இடும்பை தரும்
Pope: To make choice of one who has not been examined, and to entertain doubts respecting one who has been chosen, will produce irremediable sorrow.
Marked reading: Trusting without examining, and doubting one who has been trusted: both bring endless trouble.
Marker's nuance: The second error is the unusual one. Parimelazhagar's reason is practical: the person senses it and thinks 'this won't last', so he slackens and becomes easy for enemies to detach. Doubt creates the very betrayal it fears.
