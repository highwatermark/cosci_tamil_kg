## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:255 -> kural:507 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 507 says trusting the ignorant out of fondness brings every folly; Pazhamozhi 255 says giving high dignity to the unrestrained is putting a firebrand in a monkey's hand.
Teaches: The couplet counts the folly as the king's; the proverb shows it burning outward, since the monkey with fire harms everything near it, not only the one who handed it over.
List adds: it moves the fault from ignorance to want of restraint and adds wealth to the trust given, so the harm is power and means in an unruly hand.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:265 -> kural:691 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 691 says those who serve quarrelsome kings should be like men warming at a fire, neither too far nor too near; Pazhamozhi 265 says the experienced never even think of what would anger the mighty king, for nothing escapes his force.
Teaches: The pair shows the fire's danger from inside the servant's mind: the Kural regulates distance, and the Pazhamozhi says the only safety is never to give the king's anger cause, even when he is in the right.
List adds: its doubled proverb of deer before tiger and straw before wind adds that once the king's anger rises there is no escape at any distance, so the caution moves from posture to thought.

   2 காய்வன சிந்தியார் சுற்றறிந்தார் - பாயும்

Marked reading: The verse says that those who know the court's ways do not think of anything that would anger the mighty king even when what he does is not evil, for there is no escape for the deer before the leaping tiger, and likewise no load of straw before the wind.
