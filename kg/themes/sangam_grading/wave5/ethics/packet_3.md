## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:276 -> kural:691 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 691 asks those who serve kings to be like people warming at a fire, neither too far nor too near; Pazhamozhi 276 warns the favourite who answers the king's fierce love with his own and acts without perceiving what lies hidden.
Teaches: Together they show that the danger of being too near comes most from the king's own affection: the Kural's fire burns those who press close, and Pazhamozhi shows the courtier being drawn close by love and forgetting to keep distance.
List adds: it gives the too-near case a cause the couplet leaves out, the king's love itself, and closes it with a proverb of self-betrayal.

### kural:691: Kural 691

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:220 -> naladiyar:269 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Naladiyar 269 likens the giving of fools to rain poured into the sea while the paddy withers; Pazhamozhi 220 says leaving the hunger of kin and lovers unrelieved while helping strangers is like the black palmyra.
Teaches: Both make misdirected giving a vice, not a virtue: the Naladiyar faults giving where there is no need, Pazhamozhi names the need passed over, the hunger of one's own kin.
List adds: it names who is passed over, those who came with love and one's kin, where the quatrain gives only the image of the parched field.

Note: Nanmanikkadigai 64 says the world passes over the elder who grew old unlearned and praises the young one who learned; Pazhamozhi 6 also has a young man outdo his elders, but by birth, not study.
Teaches: Both set the able young against the doubting old, but the counted work credits learning and Pazhamozhi credits lineage, so youth's claim rests on two different grounds.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:391 -> naladiyar:6 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Naladiyar 6 says no one here has leapt clear of Death and escaped (கூற்றம் குதித்துய்ந்தார் ஈங்கில்லை) and urges the wealthy to give; Pazhamozhi 391 closes on the same words as a proverb after a list of wealth, lustre, strength, beauty, youth and birth.
