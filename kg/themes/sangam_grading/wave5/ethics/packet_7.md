## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:18 -> pazhamozhi_nanooru:19 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Verse 18 forbids answering back so that an exchange becomes a two-sided quarrel; verse 19 forbids pushing ahead of one's turn in an exchange two have begun.
Teaches: Read in sequence the two verses give a small order of debate in assembly: do not turn a speech into a contest by retort, and do not break into a contest out of turn.

Note: Naladiyar 375 uses the eel that shows one end to the snake and the other to the fish for women of double conduct; Pazhamozhi 141 calls the man who courts both friends and enemies a brand burning at both ends.
Teaches: Both books picture double dealing as one body with two ends, but the eel profits by fitting each side, while the brand burns whoever holds it, so Pazhamozhi makes the two-sided man a danger to both sides rather than a clever survivor.

   1 'நோற்றோர் மன்ற தாமே கூற்றங்

  14 பழகுவர் ஆதலோ அரிதே - முனாஅது
  15 முழவுஉறழ் திணிதோள் நெடுவேள் ஆவி
  16 பொன்னுடை நெடுநகர்ப் பொதினி அன்னநின்
  17 ஒண்கேழ் வனமுலைப் பொலிந்த
  18 நுண்பூண் ஆகம் பொருந்துதன் மறந்தே
Marked reading: The friend tells the heroine not to waste away watching the wall where she scratches off the days since her man left for a distant land, driven by the saying that those who fall at others' hands rather than to Death are blessed; even if he won Pulli's festive Venkatam, where bowmen barter elephant tusks and drink their daily toddy, he could not stay there forgetting her breast, rich as Avi's golden Pothini.
Marker's nuance: The man's journey is justified with a warriors' maxim about dying in battle rather than of sickness, so a search for wealth is dressed as heroic striving; the friend answers not by disputing the maxim but by setting a famous prize (Venkatam) against a famous city (Pothini) and letting the woman's body outweigh both.
Marker's fields: akam/puram: akam; marker's thinai: paalai; uri: pirithal

### purananuru:93 (Purananuru 93)

   9 ‘மறம் கந்து ஆக நல்லமர் வீழ்ந்த
  10 நீழ் கழல் மறவர் செல்வுழிச் செல்க! என
  11 வாள்போழ்ந்து அடக்கலும் உய்ந்தனர் மாதோ;
  12 வரிஞிமிறு ஆர்க்கும் வாய்புகு கடாஅத்து
  13 அண்ணல் யானை அடுகளத் தொழிய,
  14 அருஞ்சமம் ததைய நூறி, நீ
  15 பெருந் தகை! விழுப்புண் பட்ட மாறே.
Marked reading: Avvai asks what is left of going to war with drums booming, since the kings who could not even meet the van and ran would have died of sickness and been laid by the four-Veda priests on darbha grass and cut with a sword, so as to go where heroes go; they escaped that rite because they fell on your field, great one, where you crushed the elephants and took a noble wound.
Marker's nuance: The poem records a rite: a body that died of disease was laid on darbha grass and cut with a sword so that it could go where those who died in battle go. A death without a wound needed a wound. The turn on உய்ந்தனர் (they escaped) is ironic: being killed by Anji saved them from that disgrace. The syntax of lines 2 to 11 is compressed and the reading is not certain.
Marker's fields: akam/puram: puram; marker's thinai: vaakai
