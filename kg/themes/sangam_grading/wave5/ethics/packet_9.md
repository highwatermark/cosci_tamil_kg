## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:244 -> purananuru:184 type=AGREES
Note: Pisirandhaiyar tells the Pandya to take his due like an elephant fed paddy in balls, not one loosed in the field; Pazhamozhi 244 gives the same rule of revenue through the bee that takes and leaves.
Teaches: The Sangam poem warns one king of the ruin a greedy collection brings on him and his land; the ethics verse lifts the warning into a general rule of royal taking that should leave no mark at all.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:247 -> muthumozhikkanchi:26 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Muthumozhikkanchi 26 says those under an unjust king do not openly censure him; Pazhamozhi 247 says they have no recourse at all.
Teaches: The pair gives the reason for the silence: the people do not speak against the unjust king because, with the protector himself the taker, there is no one higher to cover them.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:297 -> kural:882 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 882 says not to fear sword-like enemies but the bond with kin-like ones; Pazhamozhi 297 gives the reason as distance, the flood afar against the debt in one's own house.
Teaches: The Kural gives the ranking of the two dangers; the Pazhamozhi explains it, since the open enemy works from outside and far off while the false friend consumes the house from within.
List adds: it sets a flood, a natural force, against a household debt, so the ranking turns on nearness rather than on the enemy's strength.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:135 -> naladiyar:110 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Naladiyar 110 says what was fixed at birth does not shrink or grow and so asks why grieve at ruin; Pazhamozhi 135 turns the same fixed term against a friend turned murderer, who cannot shorten it.
Teaches: Together they show the fixed-term doctrine working in two directions: the Naladiyar uses it to quiet grief over loss, while Pazhamozhi uses it to quiet fear of betrayal, making fate a shelter against human malice.
List adds: its proverb pictures Death as the guard of a life until its portion is eaten, so the fixed span becomes protection from a plotting friend rather than a reason for calm at ruin.
