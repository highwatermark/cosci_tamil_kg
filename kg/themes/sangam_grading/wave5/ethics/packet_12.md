## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:305 -> kural:887 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 887 says a family with inner enmity is not joined though it fits like box and lid; Pazhamozhi 305 shows how an enemy creates that enmity, by flattering one brother that the two together need no one.
Teaches: The Kural describes inner enmity from inside the house that suffers it, the proverb verse from the hand that plants it, so the pair shows both the seam and the wedge.
List adds: it supplies the act of sowing discord, with its quoted boast and bone-among-dogs image, which the couplet's picture of a split family leaves unsaid.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:82 -> kural:220 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 220 says ruin through ஒப்புரவு is worth buying even by selling oneself; Pazhamozhi 82 names the concrete form of that cost, borrowing to do what is due, and the excuse it refuses, waiting for a better time.
Teaches: Read together they show the Kural's startling price, selling oneself, as the far end of an ordinary practice the worthy actually follow: going into debt rather than postponing a duty.
List adds: its pun on கடன் as debt and duty makes the cost and the obligation one word, which the couplet keeps as ruin set against gain.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:202 -> kural:667 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 667 says not to despise anyone for their form, since the great chariot has its linchpin; Pazhamozhi 202 uses the same words (எள்ளாமை வேண்டும்) of one whose small beginnings are known in town, since the market calf may become a bull.
Teaches: The two give different grounds for the same rule: the Kural's small linchpin matters now by where it stands, while Pazhamozhi's calf matters later by what it grows into, so contempt can err about function or about time.
List adds: it moves the rule from bodily size to known origin or capital, and grounds it in growth over time rather than in present function.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:25 -> kural:720 type=AGREES
Note: Kural 720 likens holding forth before those not of one's group to nectar spilled in a dirty yard; Pazhamozhi 25 likens counsel spoken among dull men to sifting flour in the sea.
Teaches: The two waste images differ in what is lost: the Kural's nectar is fouled where it falls, Pazhamozhi's flour simply vanishes, and Pazhamozhi names the hearers as fools rather than outsiders, so the loss comes from incapacity, not from difference of group.
List adds: it narrows the couplet's 'those not of one's group' to the dull and complacent and makes the counsel itself (உறுதி), not display, what is lost.
