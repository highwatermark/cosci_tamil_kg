## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:263 -> kural:448 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 448 says a ruler without the guard of those who rebuke perishes even with no enemy; Pazhamozhi 263 says the elders' counsel that saves him is painful at first.
Teaches: The pair names the cost of the Kural's safeguard: the rebuke that keeps the king from ruin must be borne as pain before it is known as protection, which is why kings are tempted to do without it.
List adds: it adds the cost to the hearer, painful first, which the couplet's plain statement of necessity leaves out.

Note: In Akananuru 167 the man tells his heart, which is pressing him to leave for wealth, that after a sweet night with his wife in the tall house he would lie sleepless in a ruined village hall, and gives up the journey; in Natrinai 366 he tells his heart that whoever leaves her washed, flower-tied hair and soft bed in the north wind that shakes the sparrow's nest is a fool.

## LINK pazhamozhi_nanooru:112 -> kural:888 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 888 says a family with inner enmity wears itself down like gold rubbing a file; Pazhamozhi 112 says provoking the defiant within one's own house is pulling the axle-pin from inside the cart.
Teaches: Together they show inner enmity as both a slow wearing and a sudden collapse, and Pazhamozhi places its cause with the householder who provokes it, not with the enmity alone.

Teaches: The quatrain turns the knowledge of old deeds against grief, and Pazhamozhi turns it against anger at enemies, recasting the foe as a servant carrying out an order, so the same doctrine is shown ending both despair and revenge.
