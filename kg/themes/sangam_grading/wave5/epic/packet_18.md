## LINK neelakesi:260 -> kural:256 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 256 argues that no one would sell flesh if no one killed to eat; 260 presses the same point against a Buddhist who says he only eats what another killed.
Teaches: The Kural's demand argument is put to work against a named defence, the eater's pure intention, and becomes a claim about karma: the buyer's deed joins the butcher's.
Gains: epic side: the retort to the monk gets a general reason why eating implies killing, which the verse only asserts | other side: the couplet's abstract buyer is given a real opponent, a monk who claims his mind keeps him clean

### kural:256: Kural 256

## LINK neelakesi:70 -> kural:340 type=SAME_IMAGE
Note: Both picture life in a house: the Kural asks whether life, a lodger in the body, has no home of its own; the Neelakesi's sage likens beings across the states of birth to people who live together in one house and do not fear each other.
Teaches: The same lodging image serves the Kural to make the soul homeless and the Jain sage to make all souls housemates, so it can teach unrest or fearlessness depending on whether one looks at one body or at all lives.
Gains: epic side: the sage's housemates image is seen to rework a known Tamil figure of the soul as a lodger | other side: the couplet's lonely lodger gains a counter-image in which every being shares the house

## LINK silappathikaram:pukar_kandam_katturai:1-20 -> pattupattu:9:1-28 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Both praise the Chola land of Puhar through its water; the Pattinappalai grants that the sky may fail and makes the Kaveri the river that never fails, while the epic's summary sets the divine Kaveri beside a sky that itself never fails (பொய்யா வானம்).
Teaches: The Sangam poem rests Puhar's plenty on the river precisely because the sky can fail, and the epic's summary removes that concession, making the sky unfailing too in a line it reuses unchanged for the Pandya land, so the pair shows a Puhar-specific boast about the Kaveri turning into a stock praise that any crowned line receives.
Gains: epic side: the summary's unfailing sky gains a point of contrast: in the older praise of the same city it was the river, not the sky, that could be trusted | other side: the Pattinappalai's claim about the Kaveri gains a later form in which the river's distinction has been levelled into a formula shared by the three kingdoms

## LINK neelakesi:327 -> neelakesi:331 type=DEVELOPS
Note: 327 promises to establish that refusing flesh is a good deed; 331 completes it by turning the Buddhist view that image-makers and worshippers share merit into the view that killers and eaters share sin.
Teaches: Neelakesi's proof against meat is built from the opponent's own devotional practice: once merit can pass to those who stand at a distance from the act, sin can too, and the eater cannot claim to stand clear of the killing.
