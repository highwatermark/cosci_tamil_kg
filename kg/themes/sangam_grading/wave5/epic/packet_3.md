## LINK neelakesi:243 -> kundalakesi:1 type=TENSION
Note: The Kundalakesi praises the Buddha as one who kept nothing for himself and toiled only for others; Neelakesi takes the bodhisattva's gift of himself and his wives as proof of a man bound by nothing.
Teaches: The same total self-giving is the Buddhist invocation's ground of worship and the Jain debater's first charge, which shows the quarrel was over whether others (a wife) can be given as one's own.

### neelakesi:243 (Neelakesi 243, அர்க்கசந்திர வாதச் சருக்கம்)
   1 தன்னை யீந்ததும் தாரங்க ளீந்தது
   2 மன்ன தன்பொருள் கேட்டறங் கொண்டவன்
   3 மன்னு மில்லயன் மாந்தரைக் காணுமேற்
   4 பின்னைச் செய்வன பேசலு மாகுமோ.
Marked reading: She asks: when one who gave away himself and gave away his wives, and took dharma from hearing its meaning, sees the people of other households, can what he would do next even be spoken of?
Marker's nuance: The giver of self and wives is the bodhisattva of the birth stories, though the verse does not name him; she turns the highest Buddhist gift into a sign of a man for whom no bond holds. A Buddhist would read the same gift as perfected giving.
Marker's speaker: நீலகேசி (neelakesi), to அருக்க சந்திரன் (arkkachandran)
Marker's event: None
  claim (line 1, buddhist, refuted, voiced by நீலகேசி (neelakesi)): giving away one's own body and wives is a virtuous gift on the way to buddhahood
  claim (line 4, general, asserted, voiced by நீலகேசி (neelakesi)): a man who gives away his wives has shown that no bond restrains what he will do next

## LINK neelakesi:427 -> kural:356 type=QUALIFIES
Note: The Kural says those who have learned and seen the true thing reach the path of not coming back here; Mokkalan asks what makes non-return final, and Neelakesi answers that karma returns only through yoga activity, which the freed soul lacks.
Teaches: The Kural names the path of no return without saying what keeps it closed, and the debate supplies a mechanism: return needs the inflow of karma through activity, which release has ended.
Gains: epic side: The Jain answer is set against the general Tamil promise of a path of no return, showing which part of that promise it explains. | other side: The couplet's 'not coming back' is tested by the objection that a lasting freed self could be bound again.

### neelakesi:427 (Neelakesi 427, மொக்கல வாதச் சருக்கம்)
   1 பிறப்பதை வீடு மென்னேனவ்விடம் பேர்ப்பின் றென்னே
   2 னுறத்தகு வினைக டாமுமுண்மையா லொட்டு மென்னேன்
   3 மறத்தலில் யோக பாவமாசதா மீட்டு மென்ப
   4 திறப்பவும் வேண்டு கின்றேற்கெய்தல நின்சொ லென்றாள்.
Marked reading: Neelakesi says she does not call release a birth, nor say the place itself prevents return, nor that karmas cling merely because they exist; she holds that it is the unforgetting yoga activity that brings back the stain, and his words do not reach her.
Marker's nuance: யோக பாவம் is the Jain sense of yoga: the activity of body, speech and mind through which karma flows in. With that activity gone, nothing draws karma to the freed soul. Not the Saiva or Siddhar yoga of union.
Marker's speaker: நீலகேசி (neelakesi), to மொக்கலன் (mokkalan)
Marker's event: None
  claim (line 3, jain, asserted, voiced by நீலகேசி (neelakesi)): karmic stain returns only through the activity (yoga) of body, speech and mind; the freed soul without it is not bound again

### kural:356: Kural 356

## LINK neelakesi:760 -> neelakesi:516 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In 516 Neelakesi tells the Buddha that sound travels until it reaches the ear; in 760 she tells the Sankhya that sound has form and so cannot belong to space.
Teaches: The same Jain premise, that sound is matter, does the work against two opponents: it makes sound travel against the Buddhist and keeps it out of formless space against the Sankhya.

Teaches: The opponent's own praise of the soul as everywhere present becomes the premise that undoes his account of creation by union, since a thing never absent cannot newly arrive.
