## LINK neelakesi:475 -> sirupanchamoolam:69 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Sirupanchamoolam 69 binds the sin of a killing on five: the killer, the one who consented, the one who took the carcass, the cook and the eater; 475 mocks a master who sorts sin so that the killer bears it and the eater does not.
Teaches: Held together, the two show the Jain account of shared guilt as a deliberate counter-rule: the Sirupanchamoolam's five sharers are the answer to the very division of guilt the Neelakesi's Buddha is said to make.
Gains: epic side: Neelakesi's mockery gets a full rule of who shares a killing, which the verse only implies | other side: the manual's list of five is shown to be aimed at a rival doctrine that confined guilt to the killer

   4 சேடனார்க் காண்டுநா மென்றுதான் சென்றாளே.

Note: The patikam's three morals are that dharma becomes death to kings who err, that the great praise a pathini, and that former deeds come to fruit; here the pathini gives three lessons, one to each crowned king.
Teaches: The epic's late summary turns the patikam's morals into lessons addressed to the three kings and drops karma for the Chera's fulfilled vow, so the story's close weighs kingship more than the former deed the patikam put among its causes.

Note: In 194 Neelakesi argued that feeling, perception and formation are not other than knowing, so the five aggregates collapse; in 481 the Buddha concedes that many of his divisions of the five do not fit but holds the aggregates real.
Teaches: The objection made against the disciple is half admitted by the master, which lets the poet show the Buddhist defence retreating from the five divisions to a bare claim that the aggregates exist.
