## LINK kural:314 -> kural:550 type=TENSION
Note: Both use ஒறுத்தல், punish. 314 says to punish wrongdoers by shaming them with kindness; 550 says that the king punishing the worst of them with death is simply weeding a crop.
Teaches: The word keeps its form while the ethics of person and state divide.

### kural:314: Kural 314
Tamil: இன்னாசெய் தாரை ஒறுத்தல் அவர்நாண / நன்னயஞ் செய்து விடல்
Pope: The (proper) punishment to those who have done evil (to you), is to put them to shame by showing them kindness, in return and to forget both the evil and the good done on both sides.
Marked reading: The way to punish those who have hurt you is to shame them by doing them good, and then let it go.
Marker's nuance: ஒறுத்தல், to punish, is kept: retribution isn't abolished but redefined, and shame remains its instrument, so this isn't quite unconditional love. விடல் adds that one should leave the matter there, without savouring the effect.

### kural:550: Kural 550
Tamil: கொலையிற் கொடியாரை வேந்தொறுத்தல் பைங்கூழ் / களைகட் டதனொடு நேர்
Pope: For a king to punish criminals with death, is like pulling up the weeds in the green corn.
Marked reading: A king's dealing with the cruel by death is equal to weeding out the young crop.
Marker's nuance: The weeding image makes killing seem an act of care for the crop. It says nothing about who decides which are the weeds, and the next chapter opens on a king who may be one.

## LINK neelakesi:256 -> manimekalai:30:183-197 type=TENSION
Note: 256 parodies 'all is suffering' as a greedy monk's complaints; the Manimekalai teacher gives suffering as the first truth and clinging as its cause.
Teaches: The parody works by dropping the cause: the Buddhist epic ties suffering to clinging and its end to non-clinging, which the Jain song omits so the doctrine sounds like grumbling.
