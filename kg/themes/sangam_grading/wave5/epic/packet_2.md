Note: Neelakesi's fiery body enters another body like a shadow; Tirumular's light born in the womb spreads through the earthly body like a bubble in water, a shadow, and the eight take hold.
Teaches: Writers of opposed schools both picture the subtle body that passes into a new one as a shadow, which shows they agreed rebirth needs a subtle carrier and differed only on what it is made of, the Jain fiery and karmic bodies or Tirumular's eight.

## LINK neelakesi:97 -> kural:255 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 255 says hell will not open its mouth to release the flesh-eater; Neelakesi 97 shows the flesh-eater burning inside the fire's mouth, crying out.
Teaches: The couplet's closed mouth of hell, read beside the epic, becomes a place one is held and burned in, and the epic shows that the Kural's verdict falls on the eater as a sinner in his own right, not only on the killer.
Gains: epic side: the epic's charge against eating flesh gains the Kural's warrant that eating, not only killing, keeps one in hell | other side: the couplet's bare image of a mouth that will not open gains a picture of what is suffered inside it

### kural:255: Kural 255
Tamil: உண்ணாமை உள்ளது உயிர்நிலை ஊனுண்ண / அண்ணாத்தல் செய்யாது அளறு
Pope: Not to eat flesh contributes to the continuance of life; therefore if a man eat flesh, hell will not open its mouth (to let him escape out, after he has once fallen in).
Marked reading: The standing of life lies in not eating flesh; if one eats, hell doesn't open its mouth.
Marker's nuance: The image is of a mouth that has swallowed and won't open to release. Parimelazhagar offers a population argument for உயிர்நிலை: creatures that are eaten dwindle while others multiply. Kalaignar takes அளறு as a mud pit, not the hell of the older commentators.

### kural:913: Kural 913

## LINK neelakesi:193 -> manimekalai:29:43-54 type=TENSION
Note: The Buddhist teacher in the Manimekalai accepts only two means of knowledge, perception and inference; Neelakesi says a teacher who knew only through the senses and reasoning lacks subtle knowledge and his word is no scripture.
Teaches: The same restriction to perception and inference that the Buddhist offers as rigour is, from the Jain side, the proof that its founder was not omniscient and so cannot ground a scripture.
