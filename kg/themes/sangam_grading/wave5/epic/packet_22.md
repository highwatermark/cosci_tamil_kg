Note: The Manimekalai's teacher gives the collective name (body, water, country) as a Buddhist mode of speech; the Neelakesi's Buddha calls the soul such a name, like 'hand' for five fingers.
Teaches: The Jain poet gives his Buddha a doctrine the Buddhist epic itself states, so here the polemic reports Buddhist teaching recognisably before answering it.

Marker's nuance: The soul becomes a collective name, not a thing; 495 takes the same fingers (there a fist, முட்டி) and argues that what holds them together must be a bearer of qualities.
Marker's speaker: புத்தன் (buddha), to நீலகேசி (neelakesi)
