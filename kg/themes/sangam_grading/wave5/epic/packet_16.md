### kural:55: Kural 55
Tamil: தெய்வம் தொழாஅள் கொழுநன் தொழுதெழுவாள் / பெய்யெனப் பெய்யும் மழை
Pope: If she, who does not worship God, but who rising worships her husband, say, "let it rain," it will rain.
Marked reading: She who rises worshipping her husband and not a god: if she says 'rain', it rains.
Marker's nuance: The verse says flatly that she doesn't worship a god. Parimelazhagar softens this by adding 'other' gods. The line is both the strongest claim of a woman's power in the book and the most troubling, and Kalaignar's entry reads the rain as an image of fearful servitude, not of power.

## LINK neelakesi:472 -> kural:251 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 251 asks how one who eats flesh to swell his own can hold to grace; 472 grants the Buddha the greatest self-giving, even of his wife, and still faults him for not forbidding flesh.
Teaches: Grace in the Kural is lost by the eater; the Neelakesi adds a teacher's grace lost by silence, so that a life of total giving can still fail the test if it leaves others free to eat flesh.
Gains: epic side: the charge against the Buddha gets the Kural's rule that grace and flesh-eating cannot go together | other side: the couplet's test is extended from the one who eats to the one who teaches and does not forbid

### kural:251: Kural 251
