## LINK manimekalai:23:131-147 -> kural:2 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 2 asks what learning is worth if the learned do not bow at the feet of the one of pure knowledge; Manimekalai says learning that is merely learned is not learning, and names the learned as those who curb hatred, end hunger and love all creatures.
Teaches: Both refuse learning its own worth, but the couplet completes it in worship while the epic completes it in compassion and the ending of others' hunger, so the pair shows two answers to the same question of what learning is for.
Gains: epic side: a classic statement that learning needs a further end, against which her list reads as a choice of ends | other side: a Buddhist answer to its question that puts the further end in conduct toward beings rather than in reverence to a lord

## LINK neelakesi:533 -> kural:292 type=QUALIFIES
Note: 533 holds that naming a thing one thinks unreal is a lie; Kural 292 holds that even a falsehood stands as truth if it yields faultless good.
Teaches: Neelakesi's charge only works on a test of truth by match with things; on the Kural's test by effect, the Buddhist's conventional names, harming no one, would not be lies at all.
Gains: epic side: the verse's assumption about what truth is becomes visible as an assumption, one a widely read ethics book does not share | other side: the couplet's measure of truth by its fruit is shown to settle a philosophical dispute about speech that it never names

### kural:292: Kural 292

### naladiyar:101: Naladiyar 101
Tamil: பல்லாவுள் உய்த்து விடினும் குழக்கன்று / வல்லதாம் தாய்நாடிக் கோடலைத் - தொல்லைப் / பழவினையும் அன்ன தகைத்தேதற் செய்த / கிழவனை நாடிக் கொளற்கு.
Pope: Although you send forth the tender calf amid many cows, it has unerring skill to seek out its own mother. Deeds of old days have even so the power to search him out to whom their fruit pertains.
Marked reading: A young calf let loose among many cows can still find its own mother, and an old deed has the same skill in seeking out the one who did it.
Marker's nuance: The image makes the deed active: it searches, as the calf does, rather than waiting to be met. Neither the verse nor Mudaliar says the deed comes from a former birth; Shanmuganar adds that.

## LINK neelakesi:602 -> naladiyar:101 type=TENSION
Note: The Naladiyar says an old deed seeks out the one who did it (தற்செய்த கிழவனை) as a calf finds its mother among many cows; the Buddha in 602 says the doer has perished and the continuum running on from him receives the fruit.
Teaches: The quatrain's image needs a doer who is still there to be found, while the Buddha's answer removes him, so the pair shows that the Tamil ethical common sense about karma assumes a lasting self.
Gains: epic side: the Buddha's continuum is seen against the common Tamil picture of a deed finding its owner, which it has to replace | other side: the quatrain's calf-and-mother image is shown to carry an assumption, a persisting doer, that a Buddhist would deny

   1 மன்னவன் அருளால் வாசந்தவை எனும்
   2 நல் நெடுங் கூந்தல் நரை மூதாட்டி
   3 அரசற்கு ஆயினும் குமரற்கு ஆயினும்
   4 திரு நிலக் கிழமைத் தேவியர்க்கு ஆயினும்
   5 கட்டுரை விரித்தும் கற்றவை பகர்ந்தும்
   6 பட்டவை துடைக்கும் பயம் கெழு மொழியினள்
   7 இலங்கு அரி நெடுங் கண் இராசமாதேவி
   8 கலங்கு அஞ்அர் ஒழியக் கடிது சென்று எய்தி
   9 அழுது அடி வீழாது ஆய் இழை தன்னைத்
  10 தொழுது முன் நின்று தோன்ற வாழ்த்தி
  11 'கொற்றம் கொண்டு குடி புறங்காத்து
  12 செற்றத் தெவ்வர் தேஎம் தமது ஆக்கியும்
  13 தருப்பையில் கிடத்தி வாளில் போழ்ந்து
  14 "செருப் புகல் மன்னர் செல்வுழிச் செல்க" என
  15 மூத்து விளிதல் இக் குடிப் பிறந்தோர்க்கு
  16 நாப் புடைபெயராது நாணுத் தகவுடைத்தே
AFTER (context only; line 17): தன் மண் காத்தன்று பிறர் மண் கொண்டன்று
