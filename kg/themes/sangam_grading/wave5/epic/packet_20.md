## LINK neelakesi:557 -> naladiyar:48 type=TENSION
Note: Naladiyar 48 says those who praise sandal paste and garlands have not seen what the body is; 557 treats 'those who wear garlands and sandal have not found the path' as the absurd outcome of the Buddhist claim that nothing is pure.
Teaches: A Jain anthology and a Jain epic part ways on impurity: the Naladiyar finds it in the body and condemns the garland, while the Neelakesi attacks the Buddhist 'nothing at all is pure' because extended to everything it erases the difference between path and no path.
Gains: epic side: the epic's reductio is shown to aim at the word 'all', since its own tradition accepts the body's foulness in the same image of sandal and garland | other side: the quatrain's contempt for adornment is marked as a claim about the body, which the Jain debater would not let grow into a claim that everything is impure
