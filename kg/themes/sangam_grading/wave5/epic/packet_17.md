Note: Fragment 1 says enemies cannot escape except by heaping rare jewels with elephants and speaking submissively (யானையொ(டு) அரும்கலம் ... பணிந்துவழி மொழிதல்); Akananuru 124 opens with the same formula (நன்கலம் களிற்றொடு ... வணங்கினர், வழிமொழிந்து) as the condition on which a soldier waiting in camp may go home to his wife.

Note: Sivavakkiyar uses the banyan folded in its own seed as the image of Parabrahman grown within a man; Neelakesi mocks the tall palm said to be already in its seed.
Teaches: The tree-in-the-seed image that a Siddhar offers as an insight was, for a Jain debater, the weak point of the doctrine that effects pre-exist, so one image carries a mystic's claim and a logician's refutation.
Gains: epic side: the epic's reductio is shown to bear on a living image of Tamil devotion, not only on a school's technical thesis | other side: the Siddhar's image meets an objection it never answers: if the tree is in the seed, why is the hand that holds the seed not holding a tree

### kural:760: Kural 760

Marker's nuance: ஒண் does a lot of work: the claim is for bright wealth and not for any wealth. Parimelazhagar reads ஒண் as wealth gained by the right path, while Manakkudavar simply glosses it as bright. Everything else in the claim hinges on that adjective.
