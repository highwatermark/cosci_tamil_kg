Note: Akananuru 296 makes the husband's bathing in the great Vaigai with a dark girl the gossip of Koodal; Paripadal 6.26-43 shows the whole town, young and old, rich and strong, going down to play in the same river.

Note: The quatrain says an old deed seeks out its doer as a calf finds its mother among many cows; Kovalan's former deed reaches him through a goldsmith's lie, a king's hasty word and a drunken guard's sword.
Teaches: The quatrain's deed finds its doer by itself, while the epic shows it arriving through other people's free and blameworthy acts, so karma here does not excuse the hands it works through.
Gains: epic side: Kovalan's death gains the quatrain's image of the deed that cannot miss its doer, which explains why every escape fails | other side: the quatrain's calf gains a path: the seeking deed travels through other people's lies and violence
