## LINK neelakesi:745 -> neelakesi:302 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In 302 Neelakesi asked the Buddhist how formless consciousness could touch or be bound to a body with form; in 745 she asks the Sankhya how a formless soul with no contact with nature can see or experience.
Teaches: One Jain objection, that the formless cannot touch the formed, is aimed at two opposite opponents, the Buddhist who denies a soul and the Sankhya who makes it untouched, which shows the Jain body-sized soul is meant as the middle that both lack.

## LINK neelakesi:622 -> naladiyar:139 type=QUALIFIES
Note: The Naladiyar's new pot gives its water the scent of pathiri flowers, an image of the unlearned gaining sense from the learned; Neelakesi attacks the same image as the Buddha's proof of a trace that outlives the flower.
Teaches: The ethical image of borrowed fragrance only works if the scent has a lasting bearer, the flower-dust in the pot, so the Jain debater's demand to know whose the scent is shows what the Naladiyar's figure quietly assumes about company and influence.
Gains: epic side: the debate verse is seen to be contesting a well-worn Tamil figure, not a stray example, which explains why the Buddhist chose it and why the Jain must dismantle it | other side: the quatrain's simile of good company is shown to rest on a theory of how a quality passes, which the epic makes an open question between schools
