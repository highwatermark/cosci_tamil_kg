## LINK neelakesi:748 -> neelakesi:307 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In 307 Neelakesi answered that deeds carry the soul between births as wind turns a whirligig; in 748 she tells the Sankhya his soul, whether it goes or not, cannot be carried, and that its twofold deeds are its companion.
Teaches: The image of deeds moving the soul that answered Mokkalan becomes the dilemma that sinks the Sankhya: only a soul that acts and is acted on can be carried by its own deeds from birth to birth.

Note: Thirumurugatruppadai 167 gives the attending gods as a sum (நால்வேறு இயற்கைப் பதினொரு மூவரொடு); Paripadal 8.4-7 names the same thirty-three group by group: twelve, two who speak of medicine, eight, and eleven from the lord of Aathirai.

## LINK silappathikaram:11:15-30 -> kural:545 type=QUALIFIES
Note: The brahmin hails a Pandya who, when the thundering clouds withheld rain, bound them and made the harvest unfailing (மழைபிணித் தாண்ட மன்னவன்); the couplet says rain and harvest gather together in the land of the king who rules by the sceptre's nature.
Teaches: The couplet makes rain the natural consequence of just rule, while the epic's praise makes the king compel the clouds, so the same belief appears once as a law and once as a dynastic feat that this epic will test when the Pandya's sceptre bends.
Gains: epic side: the praise gains a stated rule by which the dynasty's rain-making can later be judged as a duty rather than a marvel | other side: the couplet gains a royal legend showing its abstract link between sceptre and rain told of one named line of kings

Teaches: Whether a void can be a goal one holds is exactly where they part: for the Jain debater the void offers no support, for the Siddhar it is the one thing to grasp.
Gains: epic side: the debater's objection is shown to rest on taking the void as mere absence, which another Tamil tradition denies | other side: the Siddhar's call to cling to the void is set against the strongest objection to it, that no one desires an empty release
