Teaches: The two pictures of the tilled self disagree on who holds the plough: in the Jain allegory release is the crop of one's own labour, in Tirumular the Lord does the tilling, so the shared farm figure carries the dispute between release by effort and release by grace.

## LINK soolamani:990 -> kural:1125 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 1125 has the man say he would recall her only if he forgot, but he does not know how to forget; 990 has the girl say that if she tried to forget, fate itself would drive her back to remember.
Teaches: Both deny forgetting, but the Soolamani lays the compulsion on fate, which the Kural leaves to the lover's nature.
Gains: epic side: The verse lacks the compact logic that remembering needs a prior forgetting. | other side: The couplet lacks a cause for the failure to forget; the verse supplies fate, and the girl's inward cry that the world does not know it.

### kural:1125: Kural 1125
Tamil: உள்ளுவன் மன்யான் மறப்பின் மறப்பறியேன் / ஒள்ளமர்க் கண்ணாள் குணம்
Pope: If I had forgotten her who has bright battling eyes, I would have remembered (thee); but I never forget her. (Thus says he to her maid).
Marked reading: The man, answering her friend's question whether he remembers them, says he would recall the qualities of the bright-eyed woman if he ever forgot them, but he doesn't know how to forget.
Marker's nuance: The answer is a logical joke that turns the friend's question over: remembering presupposes forgetting, so no, he never thinks of her. Parimelazhagar and Manakkudavar both mark this as the end of the man's speech.
