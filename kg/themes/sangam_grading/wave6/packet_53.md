## LINK kural:1300 -> kural:1130 type=TENSION
Note: ஏதிலர், the 'stranger', has been her word for him in the town's mouth, in 1129 and 1130. In 1300 the same word is used for a stranger, and now the one who isn't one's own is one's own heart.
Teaches: By the end, the outsider is inside.

### kural:1300: Kural 1300
Tamil: தஞ்சம் தமரல்லர் ஏதிலார் தாமுடைய / நெஞ்சம் தமரல் வழி
Pope: It is hardly possible for strangers to behave like relations, when one's own soul acts like a stranger.
Marked reading: The man, for Parimelazhagar, says that it's no wonder if strangers aren't one's own, when one's own heart isn't.
Marker's nuance: Parimelazhagar gives a witty aim: 'stranger' here is the sulking woman, who no longer seems his beloved, so if his own heart pains him for her sake, it's a small thing if she, a stranger, sulks. The other commentators don't name a speaker, and read it as a maxim.

### kural:1130: Kural 1130
Tamil: உவந்துறைவர் உள்ளத்துள் என்றும் இகந்துறைவர் / ஏதிலர் என்னும் இவ் வூர்
Pope: My lover dwells in my heart with perpetual delight; but the town says he is unloving and (therefore) dwells afar.
Marked reading: The woman says he lives joyfully in her heart always, while this town says he is loveless and lives apart.
Marker's nuance: The last words are identical to 1129's, and the refrain suggests how worn out she is by this argument. Whether her certainty is comfort or denial, the verse doesn't say.
