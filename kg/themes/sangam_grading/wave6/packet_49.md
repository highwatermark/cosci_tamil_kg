Note: Kalittokai 143's woman asks why the king who guards the world's lives as his own does not guard hers; Purananuru 186 says the world lives by its king and the king must know he is its life.
Teaches: The deserted woman takes the puram doctrine that the king is the people's life and finds its edge: a king can guard every life from war and want but not from a lover's leaving, so the akam poem marks a limit of royal protection that the puram counsel does not state.

### purananuru:186 (Purananuru 186)

Marked reading: Rice is not life and water is not life: the wide world lives by its king, so it is the duty of the king with a spear-strong army to know that he is its life.
Marker's nuance: The last clause turns praise into duty: it is not the people who must know the king is their life, but the king who must know it (அறிகை ... கடனே). Read that way, the poem binds the king rather than flattering him.

### kural:546: Kural 546
