## LINK civaka_cintamani:1167 -> kural:379 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 379 asks why those who take good fortune as good should grieve when it turns; Civakan, freed after imprisonment, neither grieves nor rejoices and ascribes both to former deeds.
Teaches: The epic answers the couplet's question with a stricter ideal: not only bearing ill as one bears good, but declining joy at good fortune too, since rescue is as much karma's working as captivity.
Gains: epic side: the hero's indifference gets a recognised ethical frame, the Kural's reasoning that what fate gives cannot be graded into welcome and unwelcome | other side: the couplet's rhetorical question gets a test case in which the good turn comes through another's hand, a god's, and is still referred to one's own deeds

## LINK civaka_cintamani:848 -> kural:376 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 376 says what is not one's own will not stay however guarded and what is one's own will not leave though thrown away; 848 says what is to come comes and what is to go goes, as the sea and river receive unasked.
Teaches: The Kural states the rule of fate about possessions, and the letter applies it to a dispossessed heir in the same breath as an offer of arms, showing fate being cited to back an effort rather than to forbid one.
