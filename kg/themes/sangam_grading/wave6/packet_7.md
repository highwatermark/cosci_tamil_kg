## LINK soolamani:73 -> kural:5 type=QUALIFIES
Note: At the princes' birth the clinging moisture of evil deeds is said to dry up for many; Kural 5 says deeds of darkness do not cling to those who delight in the Lord's praise.
Teaches: The Kural lifts the hold of deeds through the devotee's own praise, but the epic lifts it for many through the birth of a great being, so each work places the loosening of karma somewhere different.
Gains: epic side: The epic verse gets a stated condition for karma's loosening, the devotee's praise, which it does not state. | other side: Kural 5 gets a case where the hold is loosened by someone else's birth and not by the sufferer's devotion, a test of how exclusive its condition is.

### soolamani:73 (Soolamani 73, குமாரகாலச் சருக்கம்)
   1 திசையெலாத் தெளிந்தன தேவர் பொன்னகர்
   2 இசையெலாம் பெருஞ்சிறப் பியன்ற வேற்பவர்
   3 நசையெலா மவிந்தன நலியுந் தீவினைப்
   4 பசையெலாம் பறந்தன பலர்க்கு மென்பவே.
Marked reading: At their birth the quarters grow clear, the gods' city rings with praise, wishes are fulfilled and the clinging moisture of afflicting evil deeds dries up for many, the poet reports.
Marker's nuance: The closing என்பவே (they say) gives the claim as report. தீவினைப் பசை pictures karma as a moisture that makes deeds stick; the verse does not say whose deeds, only that it dries for many.
Marker's speaker: narrator
Marker's event: At the princes' birth the quarters clear and the hold of evil karma is said to dissolve.
  claim (line 3, general, reported, voiced by narrator): the birth of a great being dries up the clinging residue of harmful deeds for many

### kural:5: Kural 5
