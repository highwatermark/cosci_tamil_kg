### naladiyar:42: Naladiyar 42

Pope: If the body which, with a covering of skin above, possesses many apertures, owes its beauty only to that outward cloak that veils the false (the inner foulness), then is it fitting to say no word of lustful desire which hides the false from itself by the covering veil, but to regard that body as an undeveloped embryo.
Marked reading: If the body, full of openings beneath its skin, is handsome only through an outer covering that hides its falsity, then without speaking desire one should look at it as a bag turned inside out.
Marker's nuance: The commentators agree on the bag turned inside out; Pope instead renders பைம்மறி as 'an undeveloped embryo'. The verse asks for a change of viewpoint, not a change in the body.

Teaches: The Saiva text frees one from the sin by reaching the Lord, while the Jain verse leaves the deed to be worn away by conduct alone.
Gains: epic side: the verse lacks any Lord whose feet remove the sin | other side: the quatrain lacks an account of how long one killing travels through births

### kural:1251: Kural 1251
Tamil: காமக் கணிச்சி உடைக்கும் நிறையென்னும் / நாணுத்தாழ் வீழ்த்த கதவு
Pope: The axe of lust can break the door of chastity which is bolted with the bolt of modesty.
Marked reading: The woman says that the axe of desire breaks the door of restraint, bolted with the bar of shame.
Marker's nuance: The architecture is exact, as Parimelazhagar explains: restraint is the door that keeps what is inside from being taken, shame is the bolt, since restraint holds as long as shame does. Manakkudavar has அறிவு, 'understanding', as the door, though the verse says நிறை.
