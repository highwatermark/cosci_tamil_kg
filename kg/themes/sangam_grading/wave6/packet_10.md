Note: In Akananuru 167 the man tells his heart, which is pressing him to leave for wealth, that after a sweet night with his wife in the tall house he would lie sleepless in a ruined village hall, and gives up the journey; in Natrinai 366 he tells his heart that whoever leaves her washed, flower-tied hair and soft bed in the north wind that shakes the sparrow's nest is a fool.
