## LINK paripadal:3:63-80 -> tirumanthiram:2639 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Paripadal 3.63 says 'you are the fragrance in the flower' (பூவினுள் நாற்றம் நீ) in a run of identities; Tirumanthiram 2639 says the Lord is like the fragrance in the flower, the ghee in milk and the juice in fruit (பூவினுள் நாற்றமும் போலுளன்), mingled everywhere.
Teaches: The same image of fragrance in the flower is an identity in the Sangam hymn, where the god is the essence of each thing, and a simile in Tirumular, where the Lord is like that essence and stands mingled in things, so the pair shows the later Saiva poet keeping the image while holding the god at one remove from the world he pervades.

### tirumanthiram:2639: Tirumanthiram 2639 (tantra 8, சோதனை)
Tamil: காலினில் ஊருங் கரும்பினில் கட்டியும் / பாலினுள் நெய்யும் பழத்துள் இரதமும் / பூவினுள் நாற்றமும் போலுளன் எம்மிறை / காவலன் எங்குங் கலந்துநின் றானே.
Marked reading: Like what moves in the wind, jaggery in the sugarcane, ghee in milk, juice in fruit and fragrance in the flower, so is our Lord; the guardian stands mingled everywhere.
Marker's nuance: Each image is of something present throughout a thing but not visible in it. காலினில் ஊரும் is unclear: it may be scent carried on the wind, or the wind itself. Sengalvaraya notes Appar's பாலின் நெய்யாம் பழத்தின் இரதமாம் as a parallel.

## LINK pattupattu:10:67-94 -> akananuru:349 type=DEVELOPS
Note: The idyll says Nannan brings back treasures from those whose backs he has seen and pours his gifts on poets (சுரக்கும், 71-72) and lays waste the enemy front (முனைபாழ், 59); Akananuru 349, in a heroine's lament, names a Nannan who lays waste the front, breaks forts and pours their wealth unasked (சூழாது சுரக்கும்) on bards who come with their kin.
Teaches: An akam heroine can name Nannan's land by a two-line formula that the guide poem unfolds into a whole promise to a troupe, which shows the patron's praise circulated as a fixed epithet that love poets could assume their hearers knew, though the identity of the two Nannans is not certain.

Note: Kural 1095: the man says she does not look at him directly but smiles with one eye narrowed. Kalittokai 51: the girl says the young man looked at her from the corner of his eye as if to kill and smiled (கடைக் கண்ணால் ... நகை கூட்டம் செய்தான்).
Teaches: The Kural reads the sidelong smile as the girl's sign of consent, seen by the man; Kalittokai gives the same sign to the man, seen by the girl after she has lied for him, so the gesture of kurippu is not tied to one sex and seals a secret both have now chosen.

## POEM natrinai:59 (Natrinai 59)
COLOPHON: thinai: முல்லை | thurai: வினைமுற்றி மீள்வான்தேர்ப்பாகற்குச் சொல்லியது | poet: கபிலர் | patron: -

   3 எல்லு முயல் எறிந்த வேட்டுவன் சுவல
   4 பல் வேறு பண்டத் தொடை மறந்து இல்லத்து

   9 நுண் முகை அவிழ்ந்த புறவின்
  10 பொறை தலை மணந்தன்று உயவுமார் இனியே

## POEM ainkurunuru:8 (Ainkurunuru 8, மருதம்)
