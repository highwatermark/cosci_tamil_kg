## LINK natrinai:153 -> kural:338 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 338: the bond of body and life is like a bird that flies off leaving its nest alone. Natrinai 153: her heart has gone after him and her body, left behind, lives on by eating, like the lone man guarding a ruined town.
Teaches: The poem shows the couplet's departure without the death: the heart flies off as the life does in the Kural, yet the body stays, sustained only to keep watch over its own emptiness, so separation in love is a death the body is made to outlive.

Note: Purananuru 55.1-2 (மலைப் பெருவில் பாம்புஞாண் ... ஒரு கணை ... மூவெயில்) and Paripadal 5.24-25 (நாகம் நாணா, மலை வில்லாக, மூவகை ஆர் எயில் ஓர் அழல்-அம்பின்) give the three-forts deed in the same parts: mountain bow, serpent string, one arrow; both also turn on Siva's forehead eye.

## LINK paripadal:9:27-44 -> thiruppugazh:12 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Thiruppugazh 12, a Parankunram song, has Indra's daughter and the hunter girl both embrace Murugan's twelve shoulders; Paripadal 9 sets the same two wives at the same hill in a quarrel with blows of garlands.
Teaches: The later hymn keeps the two consorts side by side in a single embrace, while the Sangam song lets them fight over him at the same shrine, which shows the co-wives' rivalry was once part of the hill's sacred lore and was smoothed away as the pair became fixed icons of devotion.

Teaches: The same act is filed under different virtues: the kali friend calls forbearance toward fools wisdom and keeps patience for those who neglect you, while the Kural calls it strength, so this forbearance had no fixed place yet in the lists of virtues, standing under wisdom in one and strength in the other.

### kural:153: Kural 153
Tamil: இன்நம்யுள் இன்மை விருந்தொரால் வன்மையுள் / வன்மை மடவார்ப் பொறை

Note: Kural 1161: she would hide this pain, but it wells up like spring water when drawn. Natrinai 382: at evening she must hide her grief even if her life goes.

### kural:1161: Kural 1161
Tamil: மறைப்பேன்மன் யானிஃதோ நோயை இறைப்பவர்க்கு / ஊற்றுநீர் போல மிகும்
Pope: I would hide this pain from others; but it (only) swells like a spring to those who drain it.
Marked reading: The woman says she would hide this pain, but, as water does in a spring when one draws from it, it only rises.
Marker's nuance: The commentators differ quietly over what the drawing out is. Kalaignar makes concealing itself the action, so hiding is what increases it. For Parimelazhagar, the concealment simply fails, as water wells up for one who draws from a spring.

Marker's nuance: The poet's name, Vizhikkat Pethai Perungannanar, is taken from line 8, the wide-eyed doe. The stag is the one creature in the poem that does not move: everything else opens, blooms or flees, and he stands searching, which is the man's state put outside him.
