## LINK ainkurunuru:263 -> kural:1265 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Both are spoken by the woman about the pallor that the man's presence will remove: Kural 1265 wishes to see him and says the pallor will then leave; Ainkurunuru 263 says he has come and her beauty has come back.
Teaches: The Sangam poem gives as an accomplished fact, in two verbs set side by side, what the couplet can only promise, showing the restored beauty as the very event of his arrival rather than its later result.

### ainkurunuru:263 (Ainkurunuru 263, குறிஞ்சி)
COLOPHON: thinai: குறிஞ்சி | thurai: - | poet: கபிலர் | patron: - | decade: 27 கேழற் பத்து
   1 நன்பொன் அன்ன புனிறுதீர் ஏனல்
   2 கட்டளை அன்ன கேழல் மாந்தும்
   3 குன்றுகெழு நாடன் தானும்
   4 வந்தனன் வந்தன்று தோழிஎன் நலனே.
Marked reading: The heroine tells her friend that the lord of the hill land, where a boar dark as a touchstone eats the ripened millet golden as fine gold, has come himself, and with him her beauty has come back.
Marker's nuance: The two similes belong together: gold and the black touchstone on which gold is tried, so the boar on the golden field looks like gold drawn across the testing stone. The doubled verb (வந்தனன் வந்தன்று) makes his arrival and the return of her beauty a single event. A hidden reading of the boar as the lover enjoying ripe beauty would rest on a single match of mood, so it stays simile.
Marker's fields: akam/puram: akam; marker's thinai: kurinji; uri: punarthal

## LINK kuruntokai:378 -> akananuru:283 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In Akananuru 283 the friend, arranging the elopement, wishes rain to fall on the forest and cool it so the road is sweet; in Kuruntokai 378 the foster mother, after the girl has gone, prays the sun not burn and cool clouds hang over the waste.
Teaches: The same wish for a cooled desert passes from the accomplice who sends the girl to the woman who has lost her, so the pair shows the foster mother's grief taking the form of blessing, joining in the elopement she did not choose.

## LINK kuruntokai:196 -> purananuru:176 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kuruntokai 196: the husband who once called neem sweet now calls the cool spring water of Pari's Parambu hot and brackish; Purananuru 176: the poet regrets the days lost like people who scorned the clear spring of Parambu because it was in their own town.
Teaches: Read with the Purananuru, the friend's choice of Parambu's water carries its proverbial lesson: the sweetest thing is scorned when it is near, so she diagnoses the husband's turning as contempt bred by having the wife at home.

## LINK kuruntokai:58 -> kural:447 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 447: who can ruin those who keep companions that rebuke them? Kuruntokai 58: the man tells his rebuking companions (இடிக்குங் கேளிர்) that it would be well if they could hold back his sickness, but it has spread past bearing.
Teaches: The Kural makes the rebuking companion a ruler's best protection; the lover grants the rebuke its good intent and shows where it stops, since love has spread like butter on hot rock before any word could check it, so the ethic of correction meets its limit at kaamam.

## LINK patitruppattu:patikam:9 -> patitruppattu:patikam:7 type=SAME_IMAGE
Note: The seventh preface says Selvakkadungo, after his sacrifice, gave Ogandur and 'confounded the purohita' (புரோசு மயக்கி); the ninth preface uses the same phrase of Ilanjeral Irumporai (புரைஅறு கேள்விப் புரோசு மயக்கி), beside mantra worship, the installing of the spirits of the crossroads and the shanti rite.
Teaches: The repeated phrase shows that the preface-writers had a fixed slot for a king's dealings with his purohita, and the ninth preface fills it with a larger ritual programme than the seventh, so the prefaces record the growing weight given to priestly rites in the picture of Chera kingship.

## LINK kalittokai:150 -> kalittokai:16 type=TENSION
Note: In Kalittokai 16 virtue itself runs to block the man's venture for wealth; in Kalittokai 150 the friend says he left 'giving up virtue' (அறம் துறந்து) and still assures the woman that he will not prove false.
Teaches: In 16 virtue is the force that keeps him home, while in 150 the friend grants that he broke virtue by going and yet trusts his word, so the two poems split the man's duty into staying (அறம்) and keeping faith (not being பொய்), which can come apart.

## LINK kalittokai:98 -> akananuru:296 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In Akananuru 296 the friend refuses the man who bathed in the great Vaigai with a dark girl and comes home with his chest still wet; in Kalittokai 98 the man claims he only bathed in the Vaigai's new flood and the wife turns the flood itself into the courtesan.
Teaches: The Akananuru records water play in the Vaigai as the plain occasion of an affair and its gossip, while the Kalittokai husband offers that same occasion as an innocent excuse and the wife answers by making the river a figure for the woman, so a known scene of straying becomes the material for the wife's metaphor.

## LINK patitruppattu:70 -> kural:43 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 43 makes the chief duty the care of five: ancestors, gods, guests, kin and oneself; Patitruppattu 70 says the king, with his kin around him, fed the gods with sacrifice, pleased the sages with learning, put elders before his sons, and so paid the ancient debts (தொல்கடன்).
