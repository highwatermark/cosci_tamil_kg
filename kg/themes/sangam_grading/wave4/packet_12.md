## LINK kalavazhi_narpathu:36 -> akananuru:44 type=SAME_IMAGE
Note: Kalavazhi 36 sings the day the Kaveri lord took Kalumalam (கழுமலங் கொண்டநாள்), the enemy umbrellas overturned like mushrooms; Akananuru 44, a hero hurrying home after his king's war, names the Chola Senni who took Kalumalam and captured Kanaiyan (கணையன் அகப்படக் கழுமலம் தந்த) and likens the heroine to that king's rich town Kudavayil.
Teaches: Kalavazhi leaves the loser at Kalumalam unnamed and shows only the carnage, while the akam poet names the captive Kanaiyan and spends the victory as a mark of the Chola's prosperity, so together they show one battle remembered both as a field of dead and as a byword for a well-kept land.

## LINK ainkurunuru:55 -> kural:1183 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In Kural 1183 the woman says he took her grace and modesty and gave her sickness and pallor in exchange; in Ainkurunuru 55 the friend tells the man that because he desired her beauty and left her, her brow has paled for many to see.
Teaches: The Kural keeps the exchange of beauty for pallor as the woman's private complaint, while the Ainkurunuru friend turns the same exchange into public evidence held up to the man, so the pallor that is a lament in the couplet becomes an accusation in the town.

### kural:1183: Kural 1183
Tamil: சாயலும் நாணும் அவர்கொண்டார் கைம்மாறா / நோயும் பசலையும் தந்து

Marked reading: The woman says he took away her grace and modesty, and gave her sickness and pallor in exchange.
Marker's nuance: The language is commercial: கைம்மாறா, 'in exchange'. ### kural:1133: Kural 1133
Tamil: நாணொடு நல்லாண்மை பண்டுடையேன் இன்றுடையேன் / காமுற்றார் ஏறும் மடல்
Pope: Modesty and manliness were once my own; now, my own is the palmyra horse that is ridden by the lustful.
Marked reading: The man says he once had shame and sound manliness, and today he has the palmyra horse that lovers ride.
Marker's nuance: The form is an inventory of property, then and now. Parimelazhagar defines the two lost goods: shame keeps one from base acts, and manliness is not giving way to anything. Notably, it is the man who claims shame here; நாண் in the Kural isn't only a woman's virtue.

## LINK pattupattu:4:271-282 -> kural:922 type=TENSION
Note: The guide promises the bards rice toddy brewed by fisherfolk and roasted fish until they stagger; the couplet says do not drink, or let only those who do not care for the esteem of the worthy drink.
Teaches: The idyll counts drinking to the point of staggering among the best hospitality of the road, given freely by the poorest folk, while the couplet makes drink the mark of one who forfeits esteem, so the ethics book is rejecting a pleasure the oldest praise poetry offered as part of the gift.

## LINK thinaimalai_nootraimpathu:22 -> kural:1085 type=DEVELOPS
Note: The Kural's man asks whether her look is Death; the man of Thinaimalai 22 says her two eyes shot him as her brothers shoot beasts.
Teaches: The Kural names the killing look in the abstract, and Thinaimalai 22 grounds it in her people's trade, measuring her two eyes against her brothers' single killing arrow, so the deadly glance of the couplet turns out to be a hunter's weapon of the hills.

### kural:1085: Kural 1085
Tamil: கூற்றமோ கண்ணோ பிணையோ மடவரல் / நோக்கமிம் மூன்றும் உடைத்து
Pope: Is it Yama, (a pair of) eyes or a hind ?- Are not all these three in the looks of this maid ?

## LINK paripadal:6:26-43 -> akananuru:296 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Akananuru 296 makes the husband's bathing in the great Vaigai with a dark girl the gossip of Koodal; Paripadal 6.26-43 shows the whole town, young and old, rich and strong, going down to play in the same river.
Teaches: Read against the Paripadal's crowd at the fords, the Akananuru friend's charge cannot be that he bathed in the Vaigai, since everyone did; the scandal lies in whom he bathed with, so the akam reproach depends on a public festival that the akam poem itself never describes.
