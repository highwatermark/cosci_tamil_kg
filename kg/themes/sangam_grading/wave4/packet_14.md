## LINK paripadal:20:48-67 -> naladiyar:385 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Naladiyar 385 has the wife wonder how women who embrace many chests for gain can do so daily; Paripadal 20.48-51 has the wife's companion call the courtesan a bought woman whose womanhood is common to all (பெண்மைப் பொதுமைப் பிணையிலி).
Teaches: The Naladiyar's contrast of chaste wife and common woman is a private wondering, while in the Paripadal the same contrast becomes public abuse at the river, worked out as a farmer's claim on a straying ox, which shows the wife's chastity defended as a household's ownership of the husband and not only as her own shame.

### naladiyar:385: Naladiyar 385
Tamil: எஞ்ஞான்றும் எங்கணவர் எந்தோள்மேற் சேர்ந்தெழினும் / அஞ்ஞான்று கண்டேம்போல் நாணுதுமால் - எஞ்ஞான்றும் / என்னை கெழீஇயினர் கொல்லோ பெருள்நசையால் / பன்மார்பு சேர்ந்தொழுகு வார்.
Pope: ‘Whenever our spouse takes us in his embrace, we feel a timid shame as if we saw him then for the first time and they (wantons) daily, through desire of gain, submit to the embraces of many! How can this be?'
Marked reading: However often our husband rises from our shoulders, we feel as shy as if we were seeing him for the first time; how, then, do those who embrace many men's chests for gain do it every day?

## LINK kalittokai:119 -> kuruntokai:234 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Both close on the same half-line, மாலை என்மனார் மயங்கியோரே; Kuruntokai 234 says that for the lonely even dawn and day are evening, Kalittokai 119 says that evening is really the hour that undoes women's lives.
Teaches: The shared closing formula was a set rebuttal that could be turned two ways: the short poem stretches evening over the whole day, the long one narrows it to a killing hour, so 'those who call it evening are deluded' was a commonplace the poets argued with, not one poet's phrase.

Marked reading: The woman says that those who call evening the hour when the sky reddens as the sun goes and the mullai blooms as the light fails are confused: for those without a partner, the great breaking dawn when the crested cock calls through the tall house is evening too, and so is the day.
Marker's nuance: She uses the definition of evening (மாலை என்மனார், 'so they say') as a learned premise and refutes it from experience: the hour is not fixed by the sky but by the absence of a companion. It is a poem about the mutal itself, the time proper to mullai, overturned.
