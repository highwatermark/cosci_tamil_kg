## LINK paripadal:thirattu:9 -> kural:233 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 233 says nothing in the world stands undying except towering fame; thirattu 9 says Madurai's fame will stand and blossom as long as the king's hill exists (குன்றம் உண்டாகும் அளவு).
Teaches: The Kural makes fame the one thing that does not perish, while the Paripadal blessing gives even the city's fame a term measured by a hill, so the poets' praise of undying renown was bounded by the oldest lasting things of the land.

### paripadal:thirattu:9 (Paripadal thirattu:9, திரட்டு)
COLOPHON: thinai: - | thurai: - | poet: - | patron: -

Marked reading: Can Madurai ever decline, rather than stand firm and blossom in fame throughout the Tamil land fenced by cool Tamil, as long as the hill of the king with the bannered chariot endures?
Marker's nuance: The land is fenced not by mountains or sea but by its language (தண் தமிழ் வேலி). This is the first of three fragments built on one frame (thirattu 9, 10, 11): each ends 'as long as the king's ... exists', and the measure moves from the hill to the river to his word. Whose hill is meant (the Pandya's, or the god's Parankundram) the fragment does not settle.
Marker's fields: akam/puram: puram; marker's thinai: paadaan

## LINK kuruntokai:276 -> kural:541 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 541 defines justice as investigating without favour and acting after judgement toward everyone; in Kuruntokai 276 the lover thinks of taking his case to the just king's court and at once asks what would become of her.
Teaches: The lover's question shows where the Kural's impartial justice stops: a harm done without intent by an innocent girl is real but cannot be judged, so the just court is imagined only to be abandoned.

### kural:541: Kural 541
Tamil: ஓர்ந்துகண் ணோடாது இறைபுரிந்து யார்மாட்டும் / தேர்ந்துசெய் வஃதே முறை
Pope: To examine into (the crimes which may be committed), to show no favour (to any one), to desire to act with impartiality towards all, and to inflict (such punishments) as may be wisely resolved on, constitute rectitude.
Marked reading: To investigate, show no favour to anyone, hold to what is fitting for a ruler and act after consideration: that is justice.
Marker's nuance: The definition is mainly procedural, four steps with no mention of outcome. கண்ணோடாது is a strong word for a book with a whole chapter praising கண்ணோட்டம். Parimelazhagar extends யார்மாட்டும் to those dearer than life, and Manakkudavar to one's own kin.

## LINK kuruntokai:334 -> kural:1155 type=QUALIFIES
Note: In Kural 1155 the woman tells her friend to prevent the departure of the one fit for her, since once he goes union is hard; in Kuruntokai 334 she tells her friend that if he leaves, what they lose is only their one life.
Teaches: Both speak to the friend before a parting, but the couplet asks for help to stop it while the poem asks for none and names the price, her life, so the pair shows the same plea in two moods, one still bargaining and one already resigned.

### kural:1155: Kural 1155
Tamil: ஓம்பின் அமைந்தார் பிரிவோம்பல் மற்றவர் / நீங்கின் அரிதால் புணர்வு

## LINK purananuru:230 -> purananuru:227 type=SAME_IMAGE
Note: 227 tells Death it has cooked and eaten its seed (வித்துஅட்டு உண்டனை) in taking Valavan; 230 likens Death to a failing farmer who eats his seed (வித்து உண்டாஅங்கு) in taking Ezhini.
Teaches: Two poets on different kings make the same argument through the seed image, that a slain warrior was the source of Death's future harvest of enemies, which shows that taunting Death with its own loss was a recognised move in kaiyarunilai and not one poet's conceit.
