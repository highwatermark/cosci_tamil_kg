## LINK pattupattu:2:163-177 -> naladiyar:2 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Naladiyar 2 tells those who gain wealth to share its food with many at once, since wealth turns like a cart wheel; the idyll's bard passes on to others what he received on the road, and the king, weighing the instability of the world, will not dismiss his guest.
Teaches: The impermanence that the Naladiyar uses as an argument for giving is already in the idyll a motive at both ends of the gift, the bard redistributing as he goes and the king holding on to the guest, so instability argued for keeping company as well as for releasing wealth before it became a quatrain about wealth alone.

### naladiyar:2: Naladiyar 2
Tamil: துகள்தீர் பெருஞ்செல்வம் தோன்றியக்கால் தொட்டுப் / பகடு நடந்தகூழ் பல்லாரோ டுண்க / அகடுற யார்மாட்டும் நில்லாது செல்வம் / சகடக்கால் போல வரும்.
Pope: When you own ample wealth acquired by blameless means, with many sharing eat the grain that steers have trodden out! In centre poised prosperity stands with no man, but revolves like the waggon's wheel.
Marked reading: When blameless wealth comes, from that very day share its food with many, because wealth never stays level with anyone but turns like a cart wheel.
Marker's nuance: துகள்தீர் (blameless) qualifies the wealth to be shared: the verse assumes it was rightly gained. Mudaliar reads the food trodden out by beasts as a hint that others have a share in it.

## LINK kalittokai:100 -> purananuru:55 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Purananuru 55 wishes the Pandya the heat of the sun, the coolness of the moon and the giving of the rain; Kalittokai 100 praises the husband, as king, with the sun's harshness to foes, the moon's coolness to those under his parasol and the rain's giving, then says each praise is ruined by his wife's tears, bangles and pallor.
Teaches: The akam friend takes the stock triad of royal praise found in the Purananuru and makes each term hostage to a woman's body, showing that the same encomium could be turned into a reproach by asking whether its subject meets it at home.

Marked reading: Maran, foremost among kings as the single eye shines on the forehead of the blue-throated god who strung the mountain bow with a snake and burned the three forts with one arrow: though elephants, horses, chariots and warriors make an army, a king's victory has the righteous way at its root; so do not bend the sceptre for your own or kill merit because it is another's; be like the sun in valour, the moon in gentleness and the rain-cloud in giving, so that the needy want nothing, and live longer than the sands the wind heaps at Senthil.

Note: The Paripadal fragment likens Madurai to the lotus from Mayon's navel, its streets the petals and the lord's palace the pericarp; the idyll has Kanchi's tall-housed town rise like the pericarp of the lotus from the blue god's navel that bore the four-faced one.

Marked reading: Our great town is like the lotus that blossomed from Mayon's navel: its streets are the petals, the lord's palace the pericarp, its Tamil households the pollen and those who live on gifts the bees that eat it; it wakes from its safe sweet sleep only to the voices reciting the four Vedas born from the tongue of Brahma born in that flower, and not, like Vanji and Kozhi, to the cock's crow.
Marker's nuance: The flower is a map of a society: palace at the centre, households as the pollen that is the flower's wealth, and gift-seekers as the bees that feed on the households, not on the palace.
