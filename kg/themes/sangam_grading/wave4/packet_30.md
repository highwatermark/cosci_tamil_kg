## LINK kalittokai:109 -> kural:1081 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 1081: on first seeing her the man cannot tell whether she is a goddess (அணங்கு), a peahen or a woman; Kalittokai 109: the man watching the buttermilk girl reports that the town's wives, judging her fit to be an anangu to everyone (யார்க்கும் அணங்கு ஆதல் சான்றாள்), keep their husbands in and shut their doors when she comes.
Teaches: Beside the couplet, the herders' poem shows that the word அணங்கு named a real danger, a beauty that strikes whoever sees it: what is one man's private bewilderment in the Kural becomes in Kalittokai a judgment the whole town shares and acts on by guarding its men.

### kural:1081: Kural 1081
Tamil: அணங்குகொல் ஆய்மயில் கொல்லோ கனங்குழை / மாதர்கொல் மாலும் என் நெஞ்சு
Pope: Is this jewelled female a celestial, a choice peahen, or a human being ? My mind is perplexed.
Marked reading: The man, on first seeing her, cannot tell whether this woman in heavy earrings is a goddess, a rare peahen or a human being, and his heart reels.
Marker's nuance: The three guesses aren't mere compliments. Parimelazhagar ties each to evidence: her indescribable form and his distress suggest a goddess, her grace and the grove suggest a peahen, and the way she looks back suggests a woman. Kalaignar's rendering drops the goddess and gives 'beauty that torments me' instead.

## LINK ainkurunuru:121 -> natrinai:20 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Both answer a husband who denies the other woman with the same verb, கண்டிகும் (we saw): Natrinai 20's heroine describes a woman leaving his bed with crushed garland and loosened hair, Ainkurunuru 121's friend a girl leaping into the sea with her garland soaked.
Teaches: The one retort to a denial can be built two opposite ways, by showing the rival as a spent lover whose body carries his marks or as a child at play, so the 'we saw her' formula's sting lies in how the witness chooses to portray the woman, not in the seeing.

## LINK kuruntokai:283 -> kural:1064 type=TENSION
Note: Kuruntokai 283 reports the man's maxim that the life of those who have nothing is more shameful even than begging, and he leaves for wealth; Kural 1064 says the worth that will not beg even when nothing is left is too great for the world to hold. On the reading that 283 ranks poverty below begging, the two rank the same states in opposite order.
Teaches: The Kural makes the destitute man who refuses to beg the height of dignity, while the akam hero's maxim makes destitution itself the deepest shame, so the poems show that the urge to go for wealth rested on a ranking of poverty and begging the Kural explicitly reverses.

## LINK thinaimalai_nootraimpathu:107 -> kar_narpathu:11 type=SAME_IMAGE
Note: Kar Narpathu 11: the kodal puts out clusters like the raised heads of snakes, and the scrub announces his quick return. Thinaimalai 107: the kodal, fearing the kaayaa as peacocks, shrinks, and the cloud like a five-headed snake falls on my head.
Teaches: Kar Narpathu uses the snake-headed kodal as a happy sign of his return, and Thinaimalai keeps the snake likeness but lets it run on into the logic of snake and peacock, so the same image that comforted the waiting woman becomes a fable of fear.

## LINK natrinai:88 -> kural:620 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 620 says those who toil on without flagging see fate's back; in Natrinai 88 the friend tells the girl not to be stricken as though by their own old deeds but to rise, go and speak to the man.
Teaches: The Kural states for the man of affairs that effort overturns fate, and the Natrinai shows the same refusal of fate in a girl's friend inside a secret love, where the effort is speech to the man, so the rule had a domestic use, and the friend's action is an answer to the household's fatalism.

## LINK paripadal:9:45-69 -> thiruppugazh:18 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Thiruppugazh 18 names Murugan of south Parankunram as the one who took the girl the hill people gave (குன்றவர்கள் தந்தபெண்); Paripadal 9.67-69 has Valli's kin, the kurinji hill-men (குறிஞ்சிக் குன்றவர் ... வள்ளி தமர்), turn the wives' quarrel into a thumbai battle on that hill.
Teaches: In the hymn the hill people appear only as givers of the bride, while in the Paripadal they stay her partisans after the marriage and fight for her against the sky-born wife, so the pair shows Valli's marriage remembered as an alliance with her kin that carried on into the god's household.

### kural:1215: Kural 1215
Tamil: நனவினால் கண்டதூஉம் ஆங்கே கனவுந்தான் / கண்ட பொழுதே இனிது
Pope: I saw him in my waking hours, and then it was pleasant; I see him just now in my dream, and it is (equally) pleasant.
Marked reading: The woman says that what she saw in waking life was sweet only then, and likewise the dream is sweet at the time it is seen.
Marker's nuance: A strong philosophical move: both are equally over when over. Waking joy has no special standing in memory. Parimelazhagar's gloss is plain: 'the two are equal for me', and what she had before was no more than this.

  23 மதுரைப் பெரு முற்றம் போல, நின் மெய்க் கண்
  24 குதிரையோ, வீறியது?
  25 கூர் உகிர் மாண்ட குளம்பின் அது; நன்றே
  26 கோரமே - வாழி! - குதிரை;
  27 வெதிர் உழக்கு நாழியால் சேதிகைக் குத்தக்
  28 குதிரை உடல் அணி போல, நின் மெய்க் கண்
  29 குதிரையோ, கவ்வியது?
  30 சீத்தை! பயம் இன்றி ஈங்குக் கடித்தது; நன்றே
  31 வியமமே வாழி! - குதிரை;
  32 மிக நன்று; இனி அறிந்தேன், இன்று நீ ஊர்ந்த குதிரை;
  33 பெரு மணம் பண்ணி, அறத்தினில் கொண்ட
  34 பருமக் குதிரையோ அன்று; பெரும! நின்
  35 ஏதில் பெரும் பாணன் தூது ஆட, ஆங்கே ஓர்
  36 வாதத்தான் வந்த வளிக் குதிரை; ஆதி
  37 உரு அழிக்கும் அக் குதிரை ஊரல்; நீ ஊரின், பரத்தை
  38 பரியாக, வாதுவனாய், என்றும் மற்று அச்சார்த்
  39 திரி, குதிரை ஏறிய செல்.

## POEM ainkurunuru:285 (Ainkurunuru 285, குறிஞ்சி)
COLOPHON: thinai: குறிஞ்சி | thurai: - | poet: கபிலர் | patron: - | decade: 29 கிள்ளைப் பத்து
   1 பின்னிருங் கூந்தல் நன்னுதல் குறமகள்

   3 ஐவனச் சிறுகிளி கடியும் நாட
   4 வீங்குவளை நெகிழப் பிரிதல்
   5 யாங்குவல் லுநையோ ஈங்கிவள் துறந்தே.
