## LINK kuruntokai:263 -> akananuru:292 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In Akananuru 292 the girl dreads the stranger velan's rite and the kid's killing because she would be ashamed; in Kuruntokai 263 the friend protests that the same rite praises strange gods that cure nothing and brands the girl as possessed.
Teaches: Held together, the two poems show the veri rite wounding the secret love from both sides: to the girl it threatens exposure, and to the friend it threatens a false name, possession, for a love she insists is blameless.

## LINK kainnilai:21 -> kural:1249 type=TENSION
Note: Kural 1249: the woman asks her heart whom it goes out looking for, since the beloved is already within it. Kainnilai 21: the woman says her heart has gone after the man, swimming the long way through the scorched forest.
Teaches: Both women speak of a heart that leaves to follow the man, but the Kural calls that going needless because he lives inside her, while the Kainnilai takes the heart's long swim as real, so the pair sets an inward answer to absence against the older picture of the heart travelling the road.

### kainnilai:21 (Kainnilai 21, பாலை)
COLOPHON: thinai: பாலை | thurai: - | poet: புல்லங்காடனார் | patron: -
   1 ஆந்தை குறுங்கலி கொள்ளநம் ஆடவர்
   2 காய்ந்து கதிர்தெறூஉம் கானம் கடந்தார்பின்
   3 ஏந்தல் இளமுலை ஈர்எயிற்றாய் என்நெஞ்சு
   4 நீந்து நெடுவிடைச் சென்று
Marked reading: The woman tells her friend of young lifted breasts and moist teeth that, as the owl begins its short hooting and our man has crossed the forest where the rays burn and scorch, her heart goes after him, swimming the long way.
Marker's nuance: The dry forest is crossed by swimming: the heart moves through the scorched land as if through water, so the metaphor carries the effort and the danger of drowning into a waterless place. The owl's short cry marks the night falling as she speaks.
Marker's fields: akam/puram: akam; marker's thinai: paalai; uri: pirithal

### kural:1249: Kural 1249
Tamil: உள்ளத்தார் காத லவரால் உள்ளிநீ / யாருழைச் சேறியென் நெஞ்சு
Pope: O my soul! to whom would you repair, while the dear one is within yourself?
Marked reading: The woman asks her heart whom it goes looking for, since her lover is within it.
Marker's nuance: Parimelazhagar says it's laughable to go looking outside for one who is inside. To me, this resembles the later devotional complaint about seeking God outside, though this resemblance isn't something the verse or its commentators claim.

## LINK natrinai:92 -> kuruntokai:16 type=SAME_IMAGE
Note: Both open with உள்ளார் கொல்லோ தோழி, the friend asking whether the men who crossed the waste will not think of them; Kuruntokai 16 answers with a lizard calling to its mate, Natrinai 92 with a tusker winning water for its mate and calf.
Teaches: The formula comes with its answer, a male creature caring for its mate on the road the man has taken, and the pair shows that answer sharpened: a lizard's call only reminds, while a tusker that provides and then moves on mirrors the man who left to provide, so the friend's comfort lies in the likeness.

## LINK paripadal:11:93-114 -> kalittokai:98 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Paripadal 11.106-110 tells literally of a youth whom the Vaigai's current carries off while a girl takes his heart (கடும் புனல் கை வாங்க, நெஞ்சம் அவள் வாங்க); in Kalittokai 98 a husband swears he was delayed bathing in the Vaigai's new flood, and his wife answers that people saw the new flood drag him off, a flood that was a woman.
Teaches: The wife's figure of the woman as a flood that carried him off is the Paripadal's festival event turned into an accusation, so the river festival was both the occasion of a man being swept away by a look and the stock excuse for it, and the Kalittokai's irony depends on the audience knowing the Paripadal scene.

### kural:1309: Kural 1309
Tamil: நீரும் நிழலது இனிதே புலவியும் / வீழுநர் கண்ணே இனிது
Pope: Like water in the shade, dislike is delicious only in those who love.
Marked reading: The man says water is sweet in the shade, and sulking too is sweet only with those who love.
Marker's nuance: It's a reciprocal theory of sulking. A quarrel is a pleasure only inside a shared love, and without it there is only heat. Parimelazhagar applies this pointedly: she doesn't feel this for him, and so her sulk is unpleasant.
