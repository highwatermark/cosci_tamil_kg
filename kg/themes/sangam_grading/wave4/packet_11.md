## LINK kainnilai:55 -> kural:1157 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 1157: do the bangles slipping from her wrist not already proclaim that the shore lord has left? Kainnilai 55: if the shore lord leaves without embracing her, the bangles will not stay on either hand.
Teaches: The venba states as a condition what the couplet packs into a past tense, so it supports reading the Kural's 'has left' as anticipatory: the bangles slip at the mere prospect of the shore lord going.

### kural:1157: Kural 1157
Tamil: துறைவன் துறந்தமை தூற்றாகொல் முன்கை / இறைஇறவா நின்ற வளை
Pope: Do not the rings that begin to slide down my fingers forebode the separation of my lord ?
Marked reading: The woman asks if the bangles slipping past her wrist don't already announce that the lord of the shore has left her.
Marker's nuance: தூற்றா is the verb for spreading gossip, so her own body has joined the town. The past tense 'has left' is anticipatory, since the news alone has made her thin.

## LINK kuruntokai:267 -> kural:335 type=TENSION
Note: Kural 335 says that, because death stops the tongue unannounced, good deeds must be rushed to; the man of Kuruntokai 267 argues from Death's crooked, daily taking that those who know it do not leave their women to go after work and wealth.
Teaches: The same awareness of death leads to opposite conduct: the Kural spends the short life on merit done in haste, the lover on staying with her and refusing the journey, so impermanence by itself does not decide between duty and love.

### kural:335: Kural 335
Tamil: நாச்செற்று விக்குள்மேல் வாராமுன் நல்வினை / மேற்சென்று செய்யப் படும்

## LINK kural:669 -> kural:628 type=TENSION
Note: 628 prevents pain by not desiring pleasure. 669 accepts pain for the sake of the pleasure that results. One asks you to stop wanting joy and the other to work for it.
Teaches: They may differ in scope, since 628 speaks of how one meets pain and 669 of which tasks to take up, but neither verse says so.

### kural:669: Kural 669
Tamil: துன்பம் உறவரினும் செய்க துணிவாற்றி / இன்பம் பயக்கும் வினை
Pope: Though it should cause increasing sorrow (at the outset), do with firmness the act that yield bliss (in the end).
Marked reading: Even though pain closes in, do resolutely the deed that yields joy.
Marker's nuance: Parimelazhagar uses technical language to weigh momentary pain of effort (கணிகம்) against the lasting pleasure of the result (பரிணாம இன்பம்). The verse only says 'joy-yielding'.

### kural:628: Kural 628
Tamil: இன்பம் விழையான் இடும்பை இயல்பென்பான் / துன்பம் உறுதல் இலன்
Pope: That man never experiences sorrow, who does not seek for pleasure, and who considers distress to be natural (to man).
Marked reading: One who does not chase pleasure and calls suffering natural does not come to pain.
Marker's nuance: Pain is prevented by an expectation, not a defence. Parimelazhagar sees two conditions: wanting pleasure brings pain, and so does trying to ward off suffering as if it were not natural.

## LINK natrinai:212 -> kural:66 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 66 says only those who have not heard their own children's prattle call the flute and lute sweet; in Natrinai 212 the bards tune their strings on the road while at home the child's sweet voice asking to be held made the mother anxious each time.

## LINK kuruntokai:231 -> kural:1247 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 1247: the woman tells her heart to give up either desire or shame, since she cannot bear both; Kuruntokai 231: neglected by the man in her own town, she says her desire, having killed shame and lost good sense, has gone far like a loosed arrow.
Teaches: The couplet presents a choice still open between desire and shame, while the poem shows it already made and past recall, desire having killed shame on its own, so the Kural's dilemma is the moment before the poem's confession.

## LINK purananuru:359 -> naladiyar:6 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Both say give now because death is certain; the Naladiyar warns that wealth is useless after the funeral drum, 359 promises a fame that outshines the burning ground.
Teaches: The same deathbed counsel serves two motives: the Naladiyar argues from the uselessness of hoarded wealth, the Purananuru from the name left behind, so the later book drops the reward the earlier one offered.

## LINK kalittokai:27 -> kural:216 type=SAME_IMAGE
Note: Kural 216 likens the wealth of the generous man to a fruit tree ripening in the town; Kalittokai 27 likens the riverbank trees flourishing in spring to the wealth of a man who gives without stint and lives by virtue.
