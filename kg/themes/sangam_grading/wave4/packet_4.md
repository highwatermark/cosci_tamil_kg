Note: Akananuru 66: seeing the son run to him, the man cries 'நெடுந்தேர் தாங்குமதி, வலவ!' and climbs down. Ainthinai Aimpathu 28: the son stands yearning, the man sees him and drives the chariot on without a look.

## LINK kalittokai:149 -> purananuru:34 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Purananuru 34 quotes dharma that there is expiation for many crimes but none for those who kill a benefit; Kalittokai 149's friend says ingratitude to helpers will not spare a man, however good his sword.
Teaches: The rule the puram poet uses to praise a king's gifts to bards is turned in the akam poem against a lover, which shows the ethic of gratitude ran across both halves of Sangam poetry and could bind a man's private love as tightly as a patron's duty.

Marked reading: Dharma says there is expiation for those who cut a cow's udders, destroy women's unborn children or wrong their elders, but none, even if the earth is overturned, for those who kill a benefit; so, the poet tells Killivalavan, if he did not sing the king who made the bards rich after they ate millet with milk and honey and roast hare, the sun would not rise; and he blesses him to live more years than the drops the Himalayan rain-cloud pours.
Marker's nuance: The poem quotes a rule of dharma in order to apply it to the poet himself: not to praise his benefactor would be the one sin with no atonement. The poet calls himself தஞ்சம் (a humble, dependent man), and the cosmic oath (the sun would not rise) raises his debt of gratitude to the order of nature.

## LINK ainthinai_aimpathu:37 -> naladiyar:398 type=TENSION
Note: Naladiyar 398: the girl answers the friend who asks whether she can walk tomorrow behind her man through the hard forest: one who gets a great horse learns to ride it at once. Aimpathu 37: the mother asks whether the girl who faltered at play can keep pace behind her lover on the stony path. The mother doubts the feet that the girl says love will make able.
Teaches: The same question, can her feet go with him, gets opposite answers: the mother measures the girl by her weakness at childhood play, the girl by the man she has gained, so across the two books the pair stages the quarrel of the elopement between the generation that remembers and the one that goes.

## LINK kalittokai:69 -> akananuru:86 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Akananuru 86 recalls a wedding of matrons pouring water with petals and paddy and the bride curled in her new cloth (கோடிக் கலிங்கத்து ஒடுங்கினள்); Kalittokai 69 compares the swan pair to a brahmin circling the fire with his shy bride shrunk in her cloth (கலிங்கத்துள் ஒடுங்கிய).
Teaches: The shy bride in her new cloth is a shared formula, but the rite around her differs: the Akananuru wedding has matrons, water and paddy and no fire, while the Kalittokai sets her beside a Veda-reciting priest circling the fire, so the two anthologies record two wedding rites in Tamil country.

### akananuru:321 (Akananuru 321, நித்திலக்கோவை)
COLOPHON: thinai: பாலை | thurai: மகட் போக்கிய செவிலி சொல்லியது. | poet: கயமனார் | patron: -
   1 பசித்த யானைப் பழங்கண் அன்ன
   2 வறுஞ்சுனை முகந்த கோடைத் தெள்விளி
   3 விசித்துவாங்கு பறையின் விடரகத்து இயம்பக்

   5 குதிர்க்கால் இருப்பை வெண்பூ உண்ணாது
   6 ஆண்குரல் விளிக்கும் சேண்பால் வியன்சுரைப்
   7 படுமணி இனநிரை உணீஇய கோவலர்
   8 விடுநிலம் உடைத்த கலுழ்கண் கூவல்
   9 கன்றுடை மடப்பிடி களிறொடு தடவரும்
  10 புன்றலை மன்றத்து அம்குடிச் சீறூர்
  11 துணையொடு துச்சில் இருக்கும் கொல்லோ?
  12 கணையோர் அஞ்சாக் கடுங்கண் காளையொடு
  13 எல்லி முன்னுறச் செல்லும் கொல்லோ?

Marked reading: After the girl has eloped, her foster mother pictures the summer road of dry springs, a stag calling its doe to food, cowherds' muddy wells where elephants come with their calf, and asks whether the girl will rest in some hamlet shelter with her companion or walk on into the night with the bold youth, since she slipped off her pebble-filled anklets so her mother would not hear and crossed the bamboo hills.
Marker's nuance: Taking off the anklets (சிலம்பு கழீஇ) is done so their sound will not betray her, but it is also the act that marks a girl's passage to marriage; here she performs it herself, in secret, so the sign of the wedding and the sign of the flight are one act.
Marker's fields: akam/puram: akam; marker's thinai: paalai; uri: pirithal

   2 ஏந்தல் மருப்பிடைக் கைவைத்து இனனோக்கிப்
   3 பாய்ந்தெழுந்து ஓடும் பயமலை நன்னாடன்
   4 காய்ந்தான்கொல் நம்கண் கலப்பு
