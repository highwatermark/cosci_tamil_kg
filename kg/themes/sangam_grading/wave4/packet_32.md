### kural:49: Kural 49
Tamil: அறன் எனப் பட்டதே இல்வாழ்க்கை அஃதும் / பிறன்பழிப்ப தில்லாயின் நன்று

Marked reading: What is called virtue is household life, and that too is good if there is nothing in it others can blame.
Marker's nuance: The whole verse turns on what அஃதும் points to. On one reading it is a rebuke to renunciants, and on another it is a condition on householders. Manakkudavar's explanation also shows a caste assumption of his own.

## LINK natrinai:220 -> kural:214 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 214 says one who knows what is fitting (ஒப்புரவு) lives and others are counted among the dead; in Natrinai 220 the lover says the townspeople, if they knew ஒப்புரவு, would side with him and tell him not to be a stranger to the girl.
Teaches: The lover appeals to the Kural's civic duty of fellow-feeling to claim the town's support for a love match it is condemning, so the pair shows ஒப்புரவு invoked against the town's own idea of propriety.

### kural:214: Kural 214
Tamil: ஒத்த தறவோன் உயிர்வாழ்வான் மற்றையான் / செத்தாருள் வைக்கப் படும்

## LINK natrinai:155 -> kural:1081 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 1081: on first seeing her the man cannot tell whether she is a goddess, a peahen or a woman. Natrinai 155: at their second meeting he asks her aloud whether she is the goddess of the sea or of the backwater, and she answers with a smile and tears.
Teaches: The couplet leaves the man's doubt as a question in his head; the poem puts the same question to the girl and lets her answer it, since a smile and tears are a woman's reply, not a goddess's, so the doubt is settled by her response.

### kural:1081: Kural 1081
Tamil: அணங்குகொல் ஆய்மயில் கொல்லோ கனங்குழை / மாதர்கொல் மாலும் என் நெஞ்சு
Pope: Is this jewelled female a celestial, a choice peahen, or a human being ? My mind is perplexed.
Marked reading: The man, on first seeing her, cannot tell whether this woman in heavy earrings is a goddess, a rare peahen or a human being, and his heart reels.
Marker's nuance: The three guesses aren't mere compliments. Parimelazhagar ties each to evidence: her indescribable form and his distress suggest a goddess, her grace and the grove suggest a peahen, and the way she looks back suggests a woman. Kalaignar's rendering drops the goddess and gives 'beauty that torments me' instead.

## LINK kalittokai:74 -> kural:580 type=TENSION
Note: Kural 580 praises those who, wanting a refinement worth loving, see poison poured and drink it; Kalittokai 74 calls the women who trust the husband's words though they see his graceless ways deluded, like those who eat poison knowing it kills.
Teaches: Knowingly swallowing poison is a mark of courtesy in the Kural and a mark of delusion in the Kalittokai, so the same act is judged by whom it is done for: forbearance toward a friend is refinement, forbearance toward a faithless lover is folly.

### kural:580: Kural 580
Tamil: பெயக்கண்டும் நஞ்சுண் டமைவர் நயத்தக்க / நாகரிகம் வேண்டு பவர்
Pope: Those who desire (to cultivate that degree of) urbanity which all shall love, even after swallowing the poison served to them by their friends, will be friendly with them.

## LINK ainthinai_ezhupathu:34 -> kural:1247 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In Kural 1247 the woman in separation tells her heart to give up either desire or shame, since she cannot bear both; in Ainthinai Ezhupathu 34 she foresees her heart throwing off shame and going with her life after him.
Teaches: The couplet poses the choice between desire and shame as a demand on the heart, and the venba shows the heart's answer and its cost, since choosing desire there means the life leaving the body with it.

## LINK patitruppattu:21 -> kural:86 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 86 says the one who tends the departing guest and watches for the next becomes a good guest of those in the sky; Patitruppattu 21 calls the king's kitchen-fat an ஆவுதி (oblation) beside the sacrificial one and says the two smells together please the gods who abide in the sky.
Teaches: The Chera poem shows the mechanism the Kural only asserts: feeding guests reaches the gods because the kitchen smoke is itself counted as an oblation, so hospitality was understood as a second sacrifice rather than merely compared to one.
