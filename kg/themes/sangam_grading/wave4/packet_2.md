## LINK kuruntokai:301 -> kural:1179 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 1179 says her eyes do not sleep when he fails to come nor when he comes; in Kuruntokai 301 he does not come, and the bell of a chariot that is not there rings in her ear at midnight while her eyes give up sleep.
Teaches: The poem shows how the couplet's 'when he does not come' still keeps the eyes open: the ear makes his chariot out of the night, so absence is lived as an arrival that never quite happens.

## LINK pattupattu:2:60-72 -> purananuru:209 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Puram 209 compares the bard to birds that fly to a fruit tree in a mountain cleft and turn back with nothing when it is bare; the guide uses the bird and fruit tree as the image of certain success.
Teaches: The bird-and-fruit-tree simile carried the bard's risk as well as his hope, since in Puram 209 the tree can be bare, so the guide poem's use of it is a claim that this patron is never the empty tree, and its first-person testimony is offered to meet exactly that doubt.

## LINK akananuru:276 -> kural:1007 type=PARALLEL_DOMAIN
Note: Kural 1007 likens the wealth of a man who gives nothing to the needy to a beautiful woman growing old alone; the courtesan in Akananuru 276 likens her own beauty, if she fails to win the man, to the wealth of one who hoarded and never gave to those who asked.
Teaches: The two verses run the same comparison in opposite directions, beauty as vehicle for hoarded wealth in the Kural and hoarded wealth as vehicle for beauty in the poem, which shows the pairing was a shared idea in which each term could explain the other.
