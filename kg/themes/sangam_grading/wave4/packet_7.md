## LINK ainthinai_ezhupathu:63 -> kuruntokai:236 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kuruntokai 236: the friend tells the shore lord to go if he must, but first give back the beauty he consumed (நீ உண்டவென் னலனே). Ainthinai Ezhupathu 63: the heroine would accost him herself and say 'give back the colour' (வண்ணந்தா).

## LINK natrinai:194 -> kural:211 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 211: duty seeks no return, and the world makes none to the rain. Natrinai 194: the friend asks what return (கைம்மாறு) they can make to the elephant and the parrot.
Teaches: The friend applies the Kural's word for repayment to beasts who helped without meaning to, the parrot that raided the millet and so kept the girls in the field, so her mock gratitude turns the rule of unrepayable benefit into a teasing way of announcing that the meetings are over.

## LINK paripadal:1:52-68 -> purananuru:56 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Purananuru 56 makes the Pandya equal (ஒத்தீயே) to each of four gods in one quality; Paripadal 1.53-57 says no one can be found of whom to say the god is like them, so he is like only himself (நின்னோர் அனையை); both describe the bird-bannered god's body with the same formula, மண்ணுறு மணி (Pura 56.5, Pari 1.62).
Teaches: The pairing shows a limit to the பூவைநிலை way of praising a king by the gods: the likeness runs one way only, since a king can be measured by a god but the hymn finds nothing by which to measure the god.

## LINK paripadal:9:12-26 -> kural:1330 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 1330 says sulking is love's pleasure and its pleasure is the embrace that follows; Paripadal 9.12-22 ranks love, equal union and karpu, and places the courtesan, the reproach and the சுணங்கறை inside karpu, with their fruit in the sulk (ஊடல்).
Teaches: The Kural gives the sulk's worth as a man's two-line saying, while the Paripadal builds it into a ranked theory addressed to Vedic scholars and then uses it to excuse a god's wives, so the pair shows oodal already held as doctrine of Tamil porul, citable to outsiders and to the gods.

### kural:1330: Kural 1330
Tamil: ஊடுதல் காமத்திற்கு இன்பம் அதற்கின்பம் / கூடி முயங்கப் பெறின்
Pope: Dislike adds delight to love; and a hearty embrace (thereafter) will add delight to dislike.
Marked reading: The man says sulking is love's pleasure, and the pleasure of sulking is being able to come together and embrace.
Marker's nuance: In the received order, the last verse of the entire work ends on பெறின், 'if one gets to'. So 1,330 verses on virtue, wealth and love close with a chained definition and an open condition. Parimelazhagar explains the condition: knowing how far to go is the hard part, so that the union will still be possible.

## LINK natrinai:99 -> kuruntokai:66 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In Kuruntokai 66 the friend says the konrai is foolish to bloom for a stray rain before the promised season; in Natrinai 99 the friend, answering the woman's own question, says the cloud itself forgot itself and the pidavam, konrai and kodal bloomed because they are foolish.
Teaches: The Natrinai takes the Kuruntokai's single foolish tree and widens the folly to the sky and to three flowers, and places it as the answer to the woman's doubt, so the consolation grows from a remark about one tree into a whole season declared mistaken.

Teaches: Both make the evening testify against the absent man, but in opposite ways: the couplet makes it a fellow sufferer, the poem imagines a land without it, which would explain why he stays away, so the pair shows the evening serving as companion and as accusation.

### kuruntokai:330 (Kuruntokai 330)
COLOPHON: thinai: மருதம் | thurai: தலைவி கூற்று | poet: கழார்க் கீரனெயிற்றியனார் | patron: -
   1 நலத்தகைப் புலைத்தி பசைதோய்த் தெடுத்துத்
   2 தலைப்புடைப் போக்கித் தண்கயத் திட்ட
   3 நீரிற் பிரியாப் பரூஉத்திரி கடுக்கும்
   4 பேரிலைப் பகன்றைப் பொதியவிழ் வான்பூ
   5 இன்கடுங் கள்ளின் மணமில கமழும்
   6 புன்கண் மாலையும் புலம்பும்
   7 இன்றுகொல் தோழியவர் சென்ற நாட்டே.
Marked reading: The heroine asks her friend whether, in the land he went to, there is no sorrowful evening and its loneliness, when the large-leaved pakanrai opens from its bud a white flower like the thick wick a good washerwoman dipped in starch, lifted and beat and left in the cool pond, and spreads a smell that has nothing of sweet strong toddy.
Marker's nuance: The question is an accusation: if evening reached his land he would feel what she feels and come home; the scentless flower, white and limp as a starched wick, is the evening of her own field.
Marker's fields: akam/puram: akam; marker's thinai: mullai; uri: irutthal

### kural:1222: Kural 1222
Tamil: புன்கண்ணை வாழி மருள்மாலை எம்கேள்போல் / வன்கண்ண தோநின் துணை
Pope: A long life to you, O dark evening! You are sightless. Is your help-mate (also) as hard-hearted as mine.
Marked reading: The woman says to the bewildered evening, may you live, you look sad-eyed; is your partner as hard-eyed as mine?
Marker's nuance: The tender moment of the chapter: she stops fighting the hour and sees it as a fellow sufferer. Parimelazhagar explains that the pale evening's fading of light is what suggests its grief, and says she has put her own inner state onto it. The word மருள் means both the mixing of day and night and being confused.

## POEM ainthinai_aimpathu:50 (Ainthinai Aimpathu 50, நெய்தல்)
COLOPHON: thinai: நெய்தல் | thurai: - | poet: மாறன் பொறையனார் | patron: -
   1 அணிகடல் தண்சேர்ப்பன் தேர்ப்பரிமாப் பூண்ட
   2 மணிஅரவம் என்றெழுந்து போந்தேன் - கனிவிரும்பும்
   3 புள்ளரவம் கேட்டுப் பெயர்ந்தேன் ஒளியிழாய்
   4 உள்ளுருகு நெஞ்சினேன் ஆய்

## POEM kuruntokai:292 (Kuruntokai 292)
COLOPHON: thinai: குறிஞ்சி | thurai: தோழி கூற்று | poet: பரணர் | patron: -
   1 மண்ணிய சென்ற ஒண்ணுத லரிவை
   2 புனல்தரு பசுங்காய் தின்றதன் தப்பற்
   3 கொன்பதிற் றொன்பது களிற்றொ டவணிறை
   4 பொன்செய் பாவை கொடுப்பவுங் கொள்ளான்
   5 பெண்கொலை புரிந்த நன்னன் போல
   6 வரையா நிரையத்துச் செலீஇயரோ அன்னை
   7 ஒருநாள் நகைமுக விருந்தினன் வந்தெனப்
   8 பகைமுக ஊரின் துஞ்சலோ இலளே.
