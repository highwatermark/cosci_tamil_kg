## LINK natrinai:378 -> kural:791 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 791 says nothing is as ruinous as making a friend without inquiry, since the devoted cannot leave once the bond is made; Natrinai 378 ends with the friend (or the heroine) calling her sleepless nights and the neighbours' gossip the measure of the bond made without inquiry (நாடாது இயைந்த நண்பு) with the lord of the shore.
Teaches: The akam poem applies the ethics book's rule for choosing friends to a love made at a girl's sand-house, and its suffering shows the second half of the couplet at work, that she cannot leave the bond once made, so the maxim on friendship was available as a verdict on unwise love.

### kural:791: Kural 791
Tamil: நாடாது நட் டலிற் கேடில்லை நட்டபின் / வீடில்லை நட்பாள் பவர்க்கு
Pope: As those who are of a friendly nature will not forsake (a friend) after once loving (him), there is no evil so great as contracting a friendship without due inquiry.
Marked reading: Nothing brings ruin like befriending without inquiry, since for those devoted to friendship there is no leaving once it is formed.
Marker's nuance: The logic turns on faithfulness: the reason for caution is precisely the commitment to stay. Parimelazhagar adds that with no distinction between friends, the friend's sins and blame become one's own.

Teaches: The puram poet likens Ay's giving to rain, and the akam poet turns it round and likens the rain clouds to Ay's gift elephants, so the patron's bounty and the monsoon were interchangeable images and the friend can offer the season as a promise as sure as a great giver's word.

## LINK ainthinai_ezhupathu:10 -> akananuru:318 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Akananuru 318: the friend tells the man who comes alone at night by a path made fearful by elephants, snakes and tigers not to come from today, and to blow his horn on reaching home so they know he is safe. Ainthinai Ezhupathu 10: the girl asks her friend to tell the lord of the snake-haunted groves not to come by night, to keep a life safe.

## LINK natrinai:298 -> natrinai:284 type=DEVELOPS
Note: In Natrinai 284 the man already away hears heart and reason argue and fears his body will snap like a rope; in Natrinai 298 the man about to go tells his heart it cannot hold both the hills and her shoulders, and he stays.
