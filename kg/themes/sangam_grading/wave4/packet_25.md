## LINK thinaimalai_nootraimpathu:82 -> purananuru:312 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Purananuru 312 assigns each person a கடன்: bearing the son is the mother's, making him worthy the father's, and killing elephants in battle the young man's. Thinaimalai 82 calls going to make wealth the man's கடன், repeating the word three times.
Teaches: The venba carries the heroic vocabulary of duty from the battlefield to the trade road: what Purananuru 312 makes the young man's duty in war, the later akam friend makes his duty in wealth-making, and uses it to silence the wife's grief.

### thinaimalai_nootraimpathu:82 (Thinaimalai Nootraimpathu 82, பாலை)
COLOPHON: thinai: பாலை | thurai: - | poet: கணிமேதாவியார் | patron: -
   1 ஆண்கட னாம்ஆற்றை ஆயுங்கால் ஆடவர்க்குப்
   2 பூண்கடனாப் போற்றிப் புரிந்தமையால் - பூண்கடனாச்

Marked reading: The friend tells the soft-spoken heroine that men, weighing the way that is a man's duty, have taken it up and cherished it as a duty they must bear, and so will go to make wealth; it is good that she not go even a little into wasting grief.
Marker's nuance: கடன் is said three times (ஆண்கடன், பூண்கடன் twice): the comfort is an argument from obligation, which casts the heroine's grief as a lapse from her own duty to bear it.

Marked reading: To bear him and bring him up is my duty, to make him a worthy man is his father's, to forge and give him a spear is the smith's, to give him good conduct is the king's, and to cut through the hard battle with his shining sword, kill elephants and come back is the young man's duty.

## LINK natrinai:6 -> purananuru:152 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Both name வல்வில் ஓரி: Purananuru 152 shows the hunter whose single arrow kills an elephant, a tiger, a stag, a boar and an iguana, and who feeds the bard venison; in Natrinai 6 the girl's hair smells of Ori's forest, where the ripe fruit of the kumizh falls as food for young deer.
Teaches: The love poet takes from the hunter-patron's fame only his forest's scent and plenty, a place where the very deer his bow kills feed unharmed, so akam borrowed a puram name for the bounty of a land and left its violence behind.

## LINK kalittokai:27 -> kural:639 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 639 says seventy crore enemies are better than a minister at one's side who plots harm; Kalittokai 27 has spring encamp like an enemy army in the land of an unworthy king whose foolish minister let it in.
Teaches: The Kural states the danger of a bad counsellor as a maxim, while the kali shows its outcome, the enemy camped inside the land, and uses that sight for spring's assault on a lonely woman, which shows the statecraft maxim already worked as a picture poets could borrow.

Marker's fields: akam/puram: akam; marker's thinai: paalai; uri: pirithal

### kural:639: Kural 639
Tamil: பழுதெண்ணும் மந்திரியின் பக்கததுள் தெவ்வோர் / எழுபது கோடி உறும்
Pope: Far better are seventy crores of enemies (for a king) than a minister at his side who intends (his) ruin.
Marked reading: Better seventy crore enemies than a minister at one's side who plots harm.
Marker's nuance: Parimelazhagar's reason is that open enemies can be guarded against, but the one inside cannot. 'Seventy crore' is his example of a huge number, not a figure with meaning.

## LINK kuruntokai:375 -> akananuru:122 type=QUALIFIES
Note: In Akananuru 122 the girl lists the town, mother, guards, dogs, moon and owl that keep watch, and complains that when all sleep he does not come; in Kuruntokai 375 the friend says it is better he does not come, since the guards beat their drum all night against the tiger.
Teaches: The same sleepless watch is used to reproach the lover for missing his chance and to wish him away from it, so the pair shows that the catalogue of obstacles is addressed to the man in both directions, and that its real end is to make the night visit impossible and marriage necessary.

Pope: (True) friendship hastens to the rescue of the afflicted (as readily) as the hand of one whose garment is loosened (before an assembly).
Marked reading: Like the hand of a man whose garment slips, friendship clears away distress there and then.
Marker's nuance: The simile is about speed and also about no deliberation. Parimelazhagar notes that the hand gets there before the mind does. The garment also implies that the friend's trouble is a shame to be covered, and that the friend is as close as one's own limb.

## LINK kuruntokai:143 -> naladiyar:2 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Naladiyar 2: wealth stays level with no one but turns like a cart wheel, so share it the day it comes. Kuruntokai 143 uses the wealth that does not stay with a generous man as the image of pallor that will pass.
Teaches: The Naladiyar argues from wealth's instability to giving, and the love poem had already fused the two into one image, the giver whose wealth leaves him, and turned that ethical commonplace into a promise that sorrow too is the kind of thing that leaves.

   4 அறியாது அயர்ந்த அன்னைக்கு வெறி என
   5 வேலன் உரைக்கும் என்ப ஆகலின்
   6 வண்ணம் மிகுந்த அண்ணல் யானை
   7 நீர் கொள் நெடுஞ் சுனை அமைந்து வார்ந்து உறைந்து என்
   8 கண் போல் நீலம் தண் கமழ் சிறக்கும்
   9 குன்ற நாடனை உள்ளுதொறும்
  10 நெஞ்சு நடுக்குறூஉம் அவன் பண்பு தரு படரே
