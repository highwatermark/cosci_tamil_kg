Marked reading: The Pandya queen tells a companion, line by line, the omens she saw in her sleep: the umbrella and sceptre falling, the gate bell trembling, the eight directions shaking, darkness swallowing the sun, a rainbow by night and stars dropping by day.
Marker's nuance: Every omen is a disorder of the king's world or of the sky, and the first is the fall of the umbrella and sceptre, the signs of just rule; the refrain காண்பென்காண் எல்லா marks it as spoken to a companion. The lines stand in a block of their own, apart from the akaval that follows, and open with ஆங்குக், tying the scene to the moment Kannaki reaches the palace gate.
Marker's speaker: கோப்பெருந்தேவி (kopperundevi)
Marker's event: The Pandya queen tells a companion her ill-omened dream of the king's umbrella and sceptre falling.

Marker's nuance: Where the patikam and the narrator of the killing scene give former deeds as the reason the king did not inquire, the king here names only his own fault, heeding the goldsmith's word (பொன்செய் கொல்லன் தன்சொற் கேட்ட), and sentences himself; karma and the king's fault both stand as causes in the story, and he takes the second. தாழ்ந்த குடையன் தளர்ந்தசெங் கோலன் fulfils the queen's dream of the falling umbrella and sceptre. Marker's event: Kannaki breaks her anklet and a gem flies to the king's mouth; the king declares himself the thief, wishes his life ended and falls, and the queen falls at his feet.
  claim (line 74, general, reported, voiced by நெடுஞ்செழியன் (nedunjeliyan)): to kill a thief is not cruel rule but the king's due justice
  claim (line 85, general, asserted, voiced by நெடுஞ்செழியன் (nedunjeliyan)): a king who punishes on a false word is himself the thief

## LINK patitruppattu:68 -> patitruppattu:16 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Patitruppattu 16 asks whether the queen's bed will escape its loneliness while the king lingers in camp; Patitruppattu 68 shows women in the palace marking the days on the wall while the warriors fast before the fort.
Teaches: Two poets of two decades both end a war poem at the women left behind, which shows the waiting house was a set part of Chera praise, and Kapilar extends it from the queen alone to the whole palace and sets the warriors' own longings beside it.

Marked reading: In the camp pitched in a foreign land, where the drum beaten with fierce sticks roars through the sky like the sea with the wind for its drumstick, the king's men, who will not eat until they have stormed the fort, pass many days eager at heart and weary in body, and wonder whether, once they have taken the enemy's dwelling, they will carry off the white tusks of his riding elephants, pay with them in the market of fluttering toddy flags, and, more content than the nameless untroubled dwellers of the northern land, have many sweet delights each day; meanwhile, in the long-walled palace, sleepless women whose green jewels slip draw the passing days on the painted walls until their red fingers redden, women bound by the king's fragrant chest.
Marker's nuance: The poem holds two waits side by side: the warriors fasting until the fort falls and the women marking days on the wall. The akam figure of the woman counting days (compare Kural 1261) is set inside a war poem, where it measures the length of the siege. Who the wall-marking women are, the king's or the warriors', is not said; line 19 binds them to the king's chest.

Marked reading: Because the king has lingered long in his war camp, where young elephants with tusks blunted from breaking the tall gates of fortress walls hung with the ayyavi roar in musth, the poet has come to see him, and asks whether the bed of his queen, of settled chastity and gentle grace, who speaks sweetly even when she sulks and has every right to think of him, will escape its loneliness, since his soft chest, bright with gems, gold and diamonds and the garland of seven crowns, which could be the bed where her eyes sleep, now torments her.
Marker's nuance: A puram poem that turns at line 10 to the queen's waiting: the same jewelled chest that wears the seven crowns' garland (line 17, as in 14 line 11) is her pillow and the cause of her pain. பூணா ஐயவி (line 4) separates the ayyavi of the siege from the white mustard a new mother wears, a pun on one word. Lines 14 and 18 to 19 are hard to construe, so the reading of the queen's lines is tentative.

## LINK kalittokai:106 -> purananuru:278 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Purananuru 278: the old mother, told her son fled, searches the field and rejoices more than at his birth to find him cut to pieces; Kalittokai 106: the herd girls sing that they will bind the bull-gored wounds against their breasts and that the tamer's blood on their curd-spotted shoulders is an ornament.
Teaches: Akam takes over the puram pride in wounds taken facing the foe and moves it from the mother to the beloved: the wound that proves in Purananuru 278 that a son died well proves in Kalittokai 106 that a man is fit to be embraced, so the herd girls' desire is built on the warrior ethic.

## LINK pattupattu:9:1-28 -> kural:13 type=TENSION
