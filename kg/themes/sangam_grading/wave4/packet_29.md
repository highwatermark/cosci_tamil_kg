## LINK natrinai:218 -> kural:1222 type=QUALIFIES
Note: In Kural 1222 the woman asks the bewildered evening whether its partner is as hard-hearted as hers; in Natrinai 218 the girl, past the promised season, lists the evening's creatures, the bats going home, the cock calling his mate, the owl, and asks if she must hear the andril alone.
Teaches: The Kural woman makes evening a fellow sufferer, while the Natrinai girl finds every creature of the evening paired or going home, so the same projection onto nightfall can give companionship or prove isolation, and the unmarried girl gets only the second.

### kural:1222: Kural 1222
Tamil: புன்கண்ணை வாழி மருள்மாலை எம்கேள்போல் / வன்கண்ண தோநின் துணை
Pope: A long life to you, O dark evening! You are sightless. Is your help-mate (also) as hard-hearted as mine.
Marked reading: The woman says to the bewildered evening, may you live, you look sad-eyed; is your partner as hard-eyed as mine?

## LINK natrinai:16 -> kural:332 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Both hold that wealth does not stay: Kural 332 says great wealth comes like a crowd gathering at a dance and goes as it disperses; Natrinai 16 says wealth vanishes like the trail of a fish in a pond.
Teaches: The Kural uses wealth's impermanence to counsel detachment, while the Natrinai man uses it to choose his wife's gaze over a journey, so the same doctrine served an argument for staying at home in love as well as one for letting go.

### kural:332: Kural 332
Tamil: கூத்தாட்டு அவைக் குழாத் தற்றே பெருஞ்செல்வம் / போக்கும் அதுவிளிந் தற்று
Pope: The acquisition of wealth is like the gathering together of an assembly for a theatre; its expenditure is like the breaking up of that assembly.
Marked reading: The coming of great wealth is like a crowd gathering at a dance hall, and its going is like that crowd breaking up.
Marker's nuance: A crowd has its own reason for being there, and Parimelazhagar presses this: wealth comes because of good deeds, of its own accord, and leaves when that cause is spent. So the owner is no more than the hall. His addition that this includes heavenly wealth is his own.

## LINK nanmanikkadigai:70 -> kural:83 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Kural 83 says the life of one who tends guests daily does not fall into ruin; the verse says a family gains glory by the food it gives.
Teaches: The couplet answers the fear that giving food ruins a house; the verse claims more, that it is the source of the family's distinction, so feeding is shown as both safe and the very thing that ranks a house.

## LINK ainkurunuru:118 -> kural:1284 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 1284: she went to sulk, but her heart forgot it and went to unite; Ainkurunuru 118: she went meaning to rage at the unjust man and hold him back, but thought again, relented and came away.
Teaches: Both women set out angry and lose the anger on the way, but the Kural's anger turns into union while the Sangam heroine's turns into retreat, so a failed quarrel need not end in an embrace: it can end in pity and silence.

## LINK natrinai:400 -> purananuru:39 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Purananuru 39: because justice stands firm and unfailing in the Chola court at Uranthai, justice is not this king's own fame. Natrinai 400: like justice that never fails in the Uranthai court of the warlike Cholas, he knows no failing in her heart.
Teaches: The puram poet says Uranthai's justice is too settled to count as any one king's merit, and the courtesan borrows exactly that settledness for her lover's place in her heart, so an inherited institution, not a person's virtue, was the akam poet's measure of a love that cannot change.

## LINK kural:409 -> kural:972 type=TENSION
Note: 409 uses high and low birth as a working scale, even to overturn it. 972 denies the scale at the root: birth is the same for all beings, and only deeds differ.
Teaches: The two arguments against birth rank are different in kind.

### kural:409: Kural 409
Tamil: மேற்பிறந்தா ராயினும் கல்லாதார் கீழ்ப்பிறந்தும் / கற்றார் அனைத்திலர் பாடு
Pope: The unlearned, though born in a high caste, are not equal in dignity to the learned; though they may have been born in a low caste.
Marked reading: Though born high, the unlearned do not have the worth of those who are learned though born low.
Marker's nuance: The verse overturns the ranking but keeps the words மேல் and கீழ், so birth rank remains as something to be overturned. The argument is not that the distinction is false, only that learning outweighs it.

### kural:972: Kural 972
Tamil: பிறப்பொக்கும் எல்லா உயிர்க்கும் சிறப்பொவ்வா / செய்தொழில் வேற்றுமை யான்
Pope: All human beings agree as regards their birth but differ as regards their characteristics, because of the different qualities of their actions.
Marked reading: Birth is alike for all living beings, and distinction is unequal because of the difference in the work they do.
Marker's nuance: The verse says எல்லா உயிர்க்கும், wider than humanity. செய்தொழில் could be occupation or conduct, and the whole verse's social force depends on that word.

## LINK ainkurunuru:8 -> kural:545 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Ainkurunuru 8: the mother wishes that the king rule justly and there be no theft (and in poem 10, that the rains come). Kural 545: rain and harvest gather in the land of a king who rules by the sceptre's nature.

Teaches: Valluvar files the poison saying under கண்ணோட்டம; the Natrinai quotes it and then asks for exactly that kindly regard, so the pairing shows the courtesy ideal of the chapter on statecraft working in love poetry as the friend's lever to bind a man to a girl who has no one else.

## LINK paripadal:1:29-39 -> tirumanthiram:13 type=TENSION
Note: Paripadal 1.32-33 says Thirumal's coming is hard to know even for sages free of confusion; Tirumanthiram 13 puts Vishnu who measured the earth among the gods who, measuring by thought, still cannot conceive the Lord.
