## LINK neelakesi:6 -> naladiyar:151 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Both use the moon's spot: the Naladiyar to say the noble cannot bear one stain as the moon does, the Neelakesi to say a work, like the moon, may carry a fault and still be loved.
Teaches: The same image yields opposite counsel: for a person's character a single stain is unbearable, but for a poet before an assembly an acknowledged fault is excusable.
Gains: epic side: the poet's apology is seen to lower the standard the ethics books set for the noble | other side: the quatrain's rule is shown to be about moral stain, not every imperfection

### naladiyar:151: Naladiyar 151
Tamil: அங்கண் விசும்பின் அகனிலாப் பாரிக்கும் / திங்களும் சான்றோரும் ஒப்பர்மன் - திங்கள் / மறுவாற்றும், சான்றோரஃ தாற்றார் தெருமந்து / தேய்வர் ஒருமா சுறின்.
Pope: The moon that diffuses light through heaven's fair realms, and truly worthy men are alike: yet that endures a spot, while the truly worthy endure it. not; — perplexed and sad they pine away if but one stain appear.
Marked reading: The noble resemble the moon that spreads its light across the sky, except that the moon carries its spot while the noble, if one stain falls on them, pine away and waste.
Marker's nuance: The comparison turns into a contrast: the moon is the lesser because it puts up with its blemish. Sensitivity to stain is made the mark of superiority.

## LINK manimekalai:27:284-289 -> kural:354 type=QUALIFIES
Note: The Kural says the fivefold sense-knowledge is fruitless without true understanding; Manimekalai tells the materialist, who trusts the senses alone, that true things are hard to know without true understanding.
Teaches: The couplet's general claim becomes a debating move: the heroine uses its contrast between sense-knowledge and true understanding to show that a perception-only rule cannot reach even one's parentage.
Gains: epic side: Manimekalai's rebuttal gains a classical Tamil statement of the same limit on the senses | other side: the couplet gains a concrete opponent, the materialist, whom its limit refutes

### manimekalai:27:284-289 (Manimekalai, சமயக் கணக்கர் தம் திறம் கேட்ட காதை, lines 284-289)

Marked reading: Manimekalai answers that he knows his own father and mother only by inference, that without true understanding the true nature of things is hard to know, and that on his terms his own claim can be stated only as doubt; so, speaking in her assumed form, she learned the five kinds of religion.
Marker's nuance: The answer turns the materialist's rule on itself: a man who accepts perception alone cannot know his own parents, so his denial of past births is no firmer than the knowledge he calls doubt. ## LINK silappathikaram:20:92-95 -> kural:320 type=QUALIFIES
Note: The couplet says all pains rest on those who caused pain; the venba says dharma is death to the wrongdoer, but the speaker, who did no wrong, calls herself one of harsh deeds and the wrongdoer's queen suffers too.
Teaches: The epic agrees that harm returns to the one who did it, but shows it spreading to the innocent queen and to the victim who names her own karma, so the couplet's neat return of pain to its doer becomes a wider ruin.
Gains: epic side: the venba's maxim gains the couplet's general rule that harm returns to its doer | other side: the couplet gains a case where pain does reach the doer, yet also those around him, which its own wording does not consider

### kural:320: Kural 320
Tamil: நோயெல்லாம் நோய்செய்தார் மேலவாம் நோய்செய்யார் / நோயின்மை வேண்டு பவர்
Pope: Sorrow will come upon those who cause pain to others; therfore those, who desire to be free from sorrow, give no pain to others.
Marked reading: All pains rest on those who caused pain; those who want to be without pain cause none.
Marker's nuance: நோய் occurs four times. Taken strictly, 'all pains' has a hard consequence: anyone who suffers must have caused suffering. Manakkudavar accepts it by a time scheme in which present pain derives from past harm, and in that way this chapter leads into the claim of 330.

## LINK manimekalai:27:227-240 -> tirumanthiram:451 type=QUALIFIES
Note: The Sankhya speaker counts twenty-five principles with no maker; Tirumular says the Lord makes the twenty-five within the womb.
Teaches: Tirumular keeps the Sankhya count and adds a maker who shapes the twenty-five for each embryo, which is the step the Sankhya account leaves out.
