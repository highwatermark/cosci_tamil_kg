## LINK purananuru:241 -> kural:222 type=TENSION
Note: 241 presents Andiran's giving of chariots as what announces him in heaven; the Kural says giving is good even if there were no higher world.
Teaches: The Kural's hypothetical answers a real habit of praise: poets like this one tied giving to a welcome in the sky, and the couplet deliberately cuts that tie so that giving no longer needs the reward.

### purananuru:241 (Purananuru 241)
COLOPHON: thinai: பொதுவியல் | thurai: கையறுநிலை | poet: உறையூர் ஏணிச்சேரி முடமோசியார் | patron: ஆய்
   1 ‘திண்தேர் இரவலர்க்கு, ஈத்த, தண்தார்
   2 அண்டிரன் வரூஉம்’ என்ன, ஒண்தொடி
   3 வச்சிரத் தடக்கை நெடியோன் கோயிலுள்,
   4 போர்ப்புறு முரசும் கறங்க,
   5 ஆர்ப்புஎழுந் தன்றால், விசும்பி னானே.
Marked reading: Andiran, the cool-garlanded giver of sturdy chariots to suppliants, is told as arriving among the gods: at the cry that he is coming, the leather-covered drum sounds in the palace of the tall one with the thunderbolt in his great hand, and a cheer goes up in the sky.
Marker's nuance: Death is never named. The lament is turned inside out into a welcome: the only thing said of the king is that he gave chariots, and that alone is what the heavenly hall announces, so his giving is presented as his passage. The god is named only by his weapon (வச்சிரத் தடக்கை).
Marker's fields: akam/puram: puram; marker's thinai: kaanchi

### kural:222: Kural 222
Tamil: நல்லாறு எனினும் கொளல்தீது மேலுலகம் / இல்லெனினும் ஈதலே நன்று
Pope: To beg is evil, even though it were said that it is a good path (to heaven). To give is good, even though it were said that those who do so cannot obtain heaven.
Marked reading: Even if it were called a good path, taking is bad; even if there were no higher world, giving alone is good.
Marker's nuance: The verse cuts the value of giving loose from its reward by a hypothetical. How strong that hypothetical is, is exactly what the commentators disagree about.

## LINK purananuru:319 -> kural:89 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 89 calls neglect of guests poverty in the midst of wealth; in 319 the wife, with an empty jar and only roasted hare, takes in the bard and promises a golden lotus on her husband's return.
Teaches: The couplet defines the failure, poverty inside wealth, and 319 shows its converse, generosity inside poverty, where what is lacking is paid on credit against the husband's next war, so hospitality was measured by readiness to give, not by stores.

### kural:89: Kural 89
Tamil: உடைமையுள் இன்மை விருந்தோம்பல் ஓம்பா / மடமை மடவார்கண் உண்டு
Pope: That stupidity which excercises no hospitality is poverty in the midst of wealth. It is the property of the stupid.
Marked reading: Poverty in the midst of having is the folly that neglects hospitality, and it is found only among fools.
Marker's nuance: The verse redefines poverty as a failure of use, not a lack of goods. Parimelazhagar explains: since folly takes away the benefit one gets from wealth, it makes having into not having.

## LINK purananuru:234 -> kural:229 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 229 says eating alone to fill one's own store is more bitter than begging; 234 grieves that Evvi, who always ate with many at a gate open to the world, is now fed alone a small rice ball on grass by his wife.
Teaches: The Kural condemns solitary eating as the miser's choice, and 234 shows death forcing that very condition on the most open-handed host, so the bitterness the Kural assigns to the hoarder becomes, in the lament, the injury done to the giver by his funeral rite.

## LINK purananuru:84 -> kural:1065 type=SAME_IMAGE
Note: 84 says her lord has great shoulders though he eats புற்கை (coarse gruel); Kural 1065 says that even gruel thin as water (அடுபுற்கை) is sweetest when one's own effort earned it.
Teaches: The Kural's gruel of the self-reliant poor already stood in the older poem for a strong man's austere fare, cited by a woman as proof of his strength, so the couplet's dignity of plain food draws on a heroic image, not only on poverty.

### kural:1065: Kural 1065
Tamil: தெண்ணீர் அடுபுற்கை ஆயினும் தாள்தந்தது / உண்ணலின் ஊங்கினிய தில்
Pope: Even thin gruel is ambrosia to him who has obtained it by labour.
Marked reading: Though it is gruel thin as clear water, nothing is sweeter than eating what one's own effort brought.
Marker's nuance: தாள், literally the foot, stands for effort. Sweetness has become a moral quality here and doesn't describe the taste. Parimelazhagar says it's equal to ambrosia, because it's one's own.

## LINK akananuru:114 -> kural:1268 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Same situation: a man held by the king's war who longs to be home at evening. Kural 1268 says let the king fight and win, and we will hold the evening feast with the household; in Akananuru 114 the war is done and the hero urges his charioteer to reach his wife before the evening's grief.
Teaches: The couplet's two clauses, victory and then the evening at home, become in the poem a race against dusk, and the poem adds what the couplet leaves out: evening is the hour when the waiting wife suffers most, so his haste is to end her sorrow as much as to begin his feast.

## POEM akananuru:241 (Akananuru 241, மணிமிடைபவளம்)

## POEM akananuru:26 (Akananuru 26, களிற்றியானைநிரை)
