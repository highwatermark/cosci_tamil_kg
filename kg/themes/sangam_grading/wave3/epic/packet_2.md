## LINK kundalakesi:19 -> siddhar:365 type=TENSION
Note: Pattinathar asks what use weeping is, since good and evil will not come otherwise than as the lotus-born god wrote; Kundalakesi 19 has a man stay unmoved by pardon and death because all that befalls is the fruit of deeds.
Teaches: Both counsel the same steadiness, but the Siddhar grounds it in what a god wrote and the fragment in one's own deeds, so the same calm rests on incompatible accounts of who decides what comes.
Gains: epic side: the fragment's karma is set against a theistic account of the same events | other side: the Siddhar's appeal to Brahma's writing is shown to be one choice among Tamil grounds for equanimity

## LINK valayapathi:70 -> naladiyar:48 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Naladiyar 48 asks whether those who praise sandal and garlands have not seen vultures tear the corpse; Valayapathi 70 says the heart, though it sees the adorned hair burn, is still bewildered.
Teaches: The Naladiyar treats delight in adornment as ignorance that the sight of the corpse would cure, while the fragment's heart has already seen the burning and is bewildered still, so the pair separates not knowing the body's end from knowing it to no effect.
Gains: epic side: the fragment's lament is sharpened by a verse that still trusts sight to cure desire | other side: the quatrain's appeal to what one has seen is shown to have a limit, a heart that sees and is unmoved

### kural:741: Kural 741
Tamil: ஆற்று பவர்க்கும் அரண்பொருள் அஞ்சித்தற் / போற்று பவர்க்கும் பொருள்

### kural:775: Kural 775
Tamil: விழித்தகண் வேல்கொண டெறிய அழித்திமைப்பின் / ஒட்டன்றோ வன்க ணவர்க்கு

Marker's nuance: Valour is carried to the reflex. Parimelazhagar's line is that even withdrawing the angry gaze counts as retreat.
