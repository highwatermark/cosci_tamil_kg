## LINK thirikadugam:26 -> thirikadugam:34 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Both close by naming three who are called the world: verse 26 a considerate guest, a non-killer and a gentle teacher; verse 34 the brahmin, the just king and orderly people.
Teaches: The book defines 'the world' twice, once by three private virtues and once by three estates, so the word means both good persons and an ordered society.

### thirikadugam:26 (Thirikadugam 26)
   1 ஒல்வ தறியும் விருந்தினனும் ஆருயிரைக்
   2 கொல்வ திடைநீக்கி வாழ்வானும் - வல்லிதின்
   3 சீலமினி துடைய ஆசானும் இம்மூவர்
   4 ஞாலம் எனப்படு வார்
FLAG numbering: verse printed without a number; numbered 26 by its place after 25
Marked reading: The guest who knows what his host can afford, the man who lives having put away the killing of precious life, and the teacher richly possessed of sweet good conduct are named the ones who are called the world.
Marker's nuance: The guest is judged here, not the host: the courtesy asked of him is to know the measure of the house. ஞாலம் எனப்படுவார் means that such people are what 'the world' properly means, as உலகம் often means the worthy.
Marker's fields: domain: all; count word: இம்மூவர்

### thirikadugam:34 (Thirikadugam 34)
   1 மூன்று கடன்கழித்த பார்ப்பானும் ஓர்ந்து

Marked reading: The brahmin who has discharged the three debts, the king who weighs matters and does not bend justice, and the people who keep within bounds without turbulence are named the ones who are called the world.

## LINK sirupanchamoolam:58 -> kural:501 type=QUALIFIES

Teaches: Where the couplet uses wealth, pleasure and fear as temptations to test a man, the verse treats wealth, enjoyment and fearlessness as qualities he must have, and adds grace, so the same terms change from tests to credentials.
Item: 4 (item 4 of sirupanchamoolam:58's marked items)
List adds: It turns the couplet's four tests into five possessions and adds grace that survives death, which the couplet does not name.

Teaches: The shared phrase and places show that the bathing scandal at Kazhar was a set situation of marutham, which each poet then sharpened with a different puram comparison, a rout in 226 and a lost youth in 6.

## LINK elathi:39 -> kural:664 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 664 says speaking is easy and doing as spoken is hard; Elathi 39 lists four easy-hard pairs and ends that winning is easy and the word is hard.
Teaches: The same easy-hard form ranks speech oppositely in the two books, because Kural 664 means announcing a plan while Elathi means the right and truthful word, so the pair shows how much 'speech' changes with what kind of speaking is meant.
Item: 8 (item 8 of elathi:39's marked items)
List adds: It sets four easy-hard pairs in a row, death against character and victory against the word, so speech is ranked among the hard things the Kural keeps for deeds.

### elathi:39 (Elathi 39)

   2 மேவல் (எளிது)அரிது மெய்போற்றல் - ஆவதன்கண்

   4 வேறல் எளி(து)அரிது சொல்
Marked reading: Dying is easy and fullness of character hard; to reach for the good is easy and to guard truth hard; to go toward what will be is easy and to stand firm hard; to win by being clever is easy and the right word hard.
Marker's nuance: Four easy-and-hard pairs make eight things; each hard thing is the lasting form of its easy partner. Putting death among the easy things reverses the death verses earlier in the chapter. The last pair ranks speech above victory; (எளிது) in line 2 is an editorial restoration.

### kural:664: Kural 664
