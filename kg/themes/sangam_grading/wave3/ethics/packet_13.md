## LINK elathi:11 -> kural:24 type=SAME_IMAGE
Note: Kural 24 guards the five with the goad of firmness, implying elephants without naming them; Elathi 11 names the five elephants (ஐங்களிறு) that toss the one who does not cut desire.
Teaches: Elathi makes explicit the elephants the couplet only implies, and shows what happens when the goad is missing: the senses carry their rider into the hole of rebirth.
Item: 3 (item 3 of elathi:11's marked items)
List adds: It turns the goad image into a narrative of failure, the rider tossed by the five, which the couplet's image of control does not show.

## LINK iniyavai_narpathu:26 -> naladiyar:111 type=DEVELOPS
Note: Naladiyar 111 says refusing what one cannot give is no fault, but letting an asker's hope (நசை) die after keeping him waiting is worse than ingratitude; Iniyavai calls not killing the hope (நசை) of those who come sweet.
Teaches: Both judge a giver by what happens to the asker's hope, the quatrain condemning its slow killing by false promise and the verse praising its keeping, so honesty to the hope matters as much as the gift.
Item: 1 (item 1 of iniyavai_narpathu:26's marked items)
List adds: It ties keeping askers' hope to a spirit that will not live without regard and a bond that hides nothing, so generosity is grouped with self-respect rather than with promise-keeping.

### naladiyar:111: Naladiyar 111
Tamil: இசையா ஒருபொருள் இல்லென்றல் யார்க்கும் / வசையன்று வையத் தியற்கை - நசையழுங்க / நின்றோடிப் பொய்த்தல் நிரைதொடீஇ. செய்ந்நன்றி / கொன்றாரின் குற்ற முடைத்து.
Pope: O maid with many armlets graced! To answer 'no' disgraces no man, when the boon asked exceeds his means. It is the world's course. But to delay and cheat the soul faint with desire is a sin like his who 'slays' a benefit conferred.

Marker's nuance: The verse doesn't fault refusal; it faults the false promise that turns refusal into torment. Both commentators read கொன்றாரின் as a comparison, worse than ingratitude, where Pope says only 'like'.
