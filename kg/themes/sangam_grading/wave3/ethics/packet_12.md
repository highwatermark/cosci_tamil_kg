Teaches: Set beside the couplet, the verse declines the Kural's ranking of the two vows and admits wealth among the goods between them, a Jain list that values non-killing without making it the single highest good here.
Item: 3 (item 3 of sirupanchamoolam:38's marked items)
List adds: It lists not lying, wealth and not killing side by side with wealth in the middle, where the couplet orders the two vows and leaves wealth out.

## LINK elathi:4 -> kural:391 type=QUALIFIES
Note: Kural 391 says learn flawlessly and then stand by what is learned; Elathi 4 says that where six kinds of kindly conduct are seen, the books of learning may be let go.
Teaches: The Kural makes conduct the completion of learning, while Elathi makes it a substitute: together they show two orders, learning that leads to conduct and conduct that makes the books unnecessary.
List adds: It names the six acts that do the books' work (relieving distress, not scorning, shunning the base, relieving grief, avoiding blame, desired speech), so the conduct that replaces learning is specified, which 391 leaves general.

Teaches: The Kural's question reaches toward renunciation itself; the verse limits the claim to a poor or false dharma, a narrower comparison from a Jain text that honours the ascetic.
